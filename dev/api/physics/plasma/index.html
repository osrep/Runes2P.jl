Runes2P.PlasmaFromUnknowns — Type
PlasmaFromUnknowns(prescribed; temperature=:cold_electron_temperature,
                   density=:cold_electron_density, ions=nothing, temperature_floor=0.5)

A PlasmaSource whose cold-electron temperature [eV] and density [m⁻³] are the values of radial unknowns of the system at each radius, with the rest of the plasma (ions, fields) from prescribed, a PlasmaState, a function of time that returns one, or a PrescribedPlasma. density = nothing keeps the density of prescribed, which a PlasmaState built without cold_electron_density takes as the free-electron density: in a fully kinetic case the target of the e-e collisions then counts the hot and runaway electrons as well, where DREAM's target is the closure n_cold = n_free − n_hot − n_re. The values are taken without their derivative parts, so the coefficients of a term hold numbers. Their derivatives enter the Jacobian as columns of their own.

ions names the radial unknown of the densities of the ion charge states, whose components follow the species of prescribed in their order (IonComposition, ion_densities_unknown); the densities of the prescribed ions are then ignored. A plasma whose ions are unknowns takes its density from an unknown too: the free-electron density changes with the charge states, and the prescribed density would not follow it.

A temperature at or below temperature_floor [eV] raises a DomainError, which the Newton iteration reports as a failed solve and the stepper answers with a shorter step: a step that would end below the floor is refused. The default is 0.5 eV (DEFAULT_TEMPERATURE_FLOOR). DREAM has no floor, so a comparison that follows DREAM below 0.5 eV sets a lower value. The floor is also the lower limit of the temperature unknown in the Newton iteration, which damps an update that would cross it (LowerLimit, NewtonSolver). The numerical limit lies far below: the collision model needs the scaled Bessel functions of mₑc²/T, which fail above an argument of about 1e9, near 5e-4 eV.

Runes2P.ADF11Table — Type
ADF11Table

Rate coefficients of one ADF11 file of OpenADAS for the element with nuclear charge nuclear_charge, read by read_adf11:

  • class: :acd (recombination), :scd (ionization), :ccd (charge exchange), all in m³/s, :plt (line radiation) and :prb (recombination and bremsstrahlung radiation), in W m³;
  • log_density [log₁₀ m⁻³] and log_temperature [log₁₀ eV]: the nodes;
  • log_coefficient[i, j, b]: log₁₀ of the coefficient at the density node i, the temperature node j, in block b of the file (one block per charge state);
  • derivatives: the derivatives at the nodes that DREAM's bicubic interpolation uses, with respect to the log density, the log temperature, and both;
  • source: the file.

adas_coefficient evaluates a coefficient.

source
Runes2P.adas_cache_directory — Method
adas_cache_directory()

The directory of the ADF11 files: ENV["RUNES2P_ADAS_CACHE"], or ~/.cache/Runes2P/adas. It is filled by scripts/fetch_adas.py on a machine with internet access (the compute nodes of clusters often have none) and is never part of the repository.

source
Runes2P.adas_coefficient — Method
adas_coefficient(table, Z0, n, T; extrapolation=:dream)

The coefficient of table (ADF11Table) for the ion of charge Z0 at the electron density n [m⁻³] and temperature T [eV], in m³/s (W m³ for :plt and :prb). Zero where the class has no such charge state (recombination of the neutral, ionization of the bare nucleus) and at n ≤ 0 or T ≤ 0, as in DREAM.

The coefficients are those of ADAS (H. P. Summers, The ADAS User Manual, version 2.6 (2004), http://www.adas.ac.uk, with the data of OpenADAS, https://open.adas.ac.uk). The interpolation is DREAM's (src/ADASRateInterpolator.cpp): GSL's bicubic interpolation of log₁₀ of the coefficient in log₁₀ n and log₁₀ T inside the table, and outside it the bilinear interpolation of the edge cell, continued linearly (extrapolation = :dream). extrapolation = :clamp_density takes the density at the nearest edge of the table and extrapolates in the temperature alone: at low densities the coefficients tend to their coronal limit, which does not depend on the density. The default is :dream (a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package).

Derivatives with respect to n and T are exact through ForwardDiff, in place of DREAM's one-sided differences.

Runes2P.SECONDARY_SPECTRUM_WIDTHS — Constant
SECONDARY_SPECTRUM_WIDTHS

The width Ē [eV] of the energy spectrum of the secondary electrons of electron-impact ionization, dσ/dE_s ∝ 1/(1 + (E_s/Ē)²), per element, from Table II of C. B. Opal, W. K. Peterson and E. C. Beaty, J. Chem. Phys. 55, 4100 (1971), doi:10.1063/1.1676707, measured on neutral gases with primaries of 500 eV. Their fit, eq. (2), has the exponent 2.1; the exponent 2 used here is the one they allow for the integral of the spectrum (p. 4105). With it the mean over every secondary grows as ln E (argon at 1 MeV: 68.9 eV, against 44.3 eV with 2.1). The widths: helium 15.8, neon 24.2, argon 10.0, krypton 9.6 and xenon 8.7 eV; hydrogen, deuterium and tritium take the 8.3 eV of H₂. The authors call their fits to argon, krypton and xenon poor, so that "the values of Ē are not very significant for these gases"; Ē varies by about 15 % over primary energies of 100 to 2000 eV (their Table I). The widths were measured on neutral atoms and molecules with ionization energies of 9.5 to 24.6 eV and are applied to every charge state. They give no law for ions: the authors find Ē "poorly correlated with the ionization potential" (p. 4106). The default spectrum of the heating by secondaries and of the friction of bound_friction = :ionization_split is the binary-encounter-Bethe spectrum (beb_mean_secondary_energy; a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package), and these widths serve spectrum = :opal. Other elements take their width from the widths of SecondaryHeatingTerm and the secondary_widths of CollisionSettings.

Runes2P.beb_mean_secondary_energy — Method
beb_mean_secondary_energy(E, B, cut)

The mean kinetic energy [eV] of the secondary electron of an ionization of a subshell with the binding energy B by an electron of kinetic energy E, with the singly differential cross section of the binary-encounter-Bethe (BEB) model of Y.-K. Kim and M. E. Rudd, Phys. Rev. A 50, 3954 (1994), doi:10.1103/PhysRevA.50.3954, eqs. (52) to (54):

\[\frac{dσ}{dW} ∝ \sum_{n=1}^{3} F_n(t) \left[\frac{1}{(w + 1)^n} + \frac{1}{(t − w)^n}\right], \qquad F_1 = −\frac{F_2}{t + 1}, \quad F_2 = 2 − Q, \quad F_3 = Q \ln t,\]

with t = E/B and w = W/B, up to the smaller of (t − 1)/2 and cut/B, conditional on lying there, in closed form. The common factor of the F_n (1/(t + u + 1), or 1/(t + 1) for ions with open-shell valence electrons, their Sec. VI) cancels in the mean, so the spectrum needs only B. Q = 1, their approximation where the dipole oscillator strength is not known (Sec. IV), also for the one-electron ions, for which their eq. (60) gives Q = 0.5668: there the dipole term, which lies at small w, is too large, and the mean too low. Zero below the threshold. Within 1e-6 B of the threshold, where the closed form loses its digits, the mean is that of the flat spectrum there, W_top/2. The mean of a charge state takes the valence shell alone, with B its ionization energy; inner shells, which Kim and Rudd sum over, are left out. Normalized by the same integral, the spectrum gives their total cross section of the BEB model, (ln t/2)(1 − 1/t²) + 1 − 1/t − ln t/(t + 1) at Q = 1, which the tests check.

Runes2P.ionization_cross_section — Method
ionization_cross_section(element, Z0, p; form=:chianti)

The cross section [m²] of the ionization of the ion of element in charge state Z0 by an electron of momentum p (in units of mₑc), DREAM's EvaluateBCGSingleSubshell (src/Equations/Fluid/IonKineticIonizationTerm.cpp): the formula of A. Burgess and M. C. Chidichimo, Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc. 203, 1269 (1983), doi:10.1093/mnras/203.4.1269,

\[σ_{BC} = C π a_0^2 \left(\frac{R_y}{I}\right)^2 \frac{\ln(U)^{1 + β^*/U}}{U}\]

with U = E/I and the kinetic energy E, blended into DREAM's form of the relativistic Bethe formula of N. A. Garland et al., Phys. Plasmas 27, 040702 (2020), doi:10.1063/5.0003638, C π a₀² α² (R_y/I) (ln(p²/2I) − β²) with I in units of mₑc² (IonKineticIonizationTerm.cpp:221-223), by the weight S = 1/(1 + exp(1 − E/100 keV)) of DREAM's code (:224). That form lacks the factor 1/β² of a Bethe cross section: it is β² times the continuation that joins the large-U limit of σ_{BC}, so it is about 1e-4 of σ_{BC} at thermal energies, while S gives it the weight 0.27 already at E = 0. The fitted C absorbs that weight at the energies of the fit, and the shape at hot-tail and runaway energies differs from a consistent blend. Zero below the threshold U < 1 and for the bare nucleus. The parameters are those of KINETIC_IONIZATION_FITS.

form selects the cross section:

  • :chianti (the default, a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package): the parameters of CHIANTI, continued relativistically by the Bethe theory (chianti_cross_section, CHIANTI_IONIZATION_FITS), for hydrogen, its isotopes (with the parameters of hydrogen), neon and argon. The other elements, tungsten among them, keep DREAM's form below: the table holds no CHIANTI data for them.
  • :dream: DREAM's form above, with DREAM's fits. From 0.1 to 1 keV it lies within 7 % of the measurements of hydrogen (Shah et al. 1987), and at 0.5 to 1 keV at 0.75 of the single ionization of argon (Straub et al. 1995); at 1 MeV it is 1.72 times the relativistic Bethe cross section of hydrogen and grows with the energy.
  • :bethe: the term divided by β², C π a₀² α² (R_y/I)(ln(p²/2I) − β²)/β², which joins the large-U limit of σ_{BC}, with the parameters KINETIC_IONIZATION_BETHE_FITS refitted by DREAM's method. The blend is floored at zero: just above the threshold of a state with I of several keV the logarithm ln(p²/2I) lies below β² while S is already 0.3 at 15 keV, so the blend would be negative there (W⁶⁴⁺, I = 15.6 keV).

Runes2P.chianti_cross_section — Method
chianti_cross_section(fit::ChiantiIonization, p)

The cross section [m²] of the ionization of the ion of fit by an electron of momentum p (in units of mₑc): CHIANTI's direct ionization and excitation-autoionization (K. P. Dere, Astron. Astrophys. 466, 771 (2007), doi:10.1051/0004-6361:20066728), continued relativistically by the Bethe theory.

The relativistic Bethe cross section of M. Inokuti, Rev. Mod. Phys. 43, 297 (1971), doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.43.297, eq. (4.26), for an electron (z = 1),

\[σ_B = \frac{4π a_0^2 R_y}{T} \left\{M^2 \left[\ln\frac{β^2}{1 - β^2} - β^2\right] + C\right\}, \qquad T = \frac{m_e v^2}{2},\]

splits, by Fano's analysis (Inokuti, p. 328), into the nonrelativistic Bethe form (his eq. (4.18)) at the energy T = mₑv²/2 and the relativistic rise (4π a₀² R_y/T) M² [−ln(1 − β²) − β²]. The fits of CHIANTI are nonrelativistic: their high-energy limit is the nonrelativistic Bethe limit (Dere, Sect. 2.1). They are continued in the same way:

  • each shell and each transition of the autoionization is evaluated at the speed of the electron, u = β²/β_I² with β_I the speed of an electron of the threshold energy, which is E/I at low energies and keeps the threshold at E = I;
  • the relativistic rise is added with the coefficient M² of the high-energy limit of each shell and allowed transition (ChiantiIonization), weighted by 1 − 1/u so that it starts at zero at the threshold of each (a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package).

Above CHIANTI_MATCHING_ENERGY the cross section is σ_B with the M² of the fit and the C at which σ_B equals the continued fit at that energy, joined to it by the weight of CHIANTI_MATCHING_WIDTH. Below about 10 keV the continuation changes the fit by less than the spread of the data: by 0.3 % at 1 keV and about 3 % at 10 keV for the neutral atoms.

Implied by the fits: M² = 0.2842 for H (0.2823 from the fit of M. B. Shah, D. S. Elliott, H. B. Gilbody, J. Phys. B 20, 3501 (1987)), 4.180 for Ar (4.22, F. F. Rieke, W. Prepejchal, Phys. Rev. A 6, 1507 (1972), Table II) and 1.705 for Ne (2.02, the same table, 16 % low, within Dere's caveat of the Bethe limit, ChiantiDirectShell); C = 3.92 for H (4.007) and 40.8 for Ar (37.93).

The threshold is CHIANTI's ionization energy, which differs from that of ionization_energy (NIST) by up to 1.4 eV for the argon ions; the energy loss of the friction of bound_friction = :ionization_split takes NIST's. Generic in the number type of p.

Runes2P.ChiantiAutoionization — Type
ChiantiAutoionization(kind, oscillator_strength, energy, scale, values)

One transition of the excitation-autoionization of an ion in CHIANTI's .easplom files (K. P. Dere, Astron. Astrophys. 466, 771 (2007), Sect. 2.2): the Burgess-Tully type of the transition (kind), its weighted oscillator strength, its excitation energy [Ry, the unit of the files] (energy), the Burgess-Tully parameter C (scale) and the five spline nodes of the scaled collision strength at equally spaced scaled energies from 0 to 1 (values). With X = E/ΔE, the collision strength Ω is, by type, as ChiantiPy's util.splomDescale evaluates it:

  • type 1: Ω = Ω_s(1 − ln C/ln(X − 1 + C)) ln(X − 1 + e);
  • type 2: Ω = Ω_s((X − 1)/(X − 1 + C));
  • type 3: Ω = Ω_s((X − 1)/(X − 1 + C))/X²;
  • type 4: Ω = Ω_s(1 − ln C/ln(X − 1 + C)) ln(X − 1 + C).

The cross section is s π a₀² Ω/(E/R_y) with the scale s of the ion (ChiantiIonization); the statistical weight of the ground level is in Ω (ChiantiPy's Ion.eaCross). Types 1 and 4, the allowed transitions, grow as ln X and add s Ω_s(1)/4 to the Bethe coefficient M² of the ion.

Runes2P.ChiantiDirectShell — Type
ChiantiDirectShell(scale, threshold, knots, values)

The fit of the direct ionization of one shell of an ion in CHIANTI's .diparams files (K. P. Dere, Astron. Astrophys. 466, 771 (2007), doi:10.1051/0004-6361:20066728, Sect. 2.1): the Burgess-Tully parameter f (scale), the ionization energy of the shell [eV] (threshold), and the spline nodes of the scaled cross section Σ (values, in 1e-14 cm² eV², the unit of the files) at the scaled energies U (knots, from 0 to 1). Dere's eqs. (2) and (3):

\[U = 1 - \frac{\ln f}{\ln(u - 1 + f)}, \qquad Σ = \frac{u σ I^2}{\ln u + 1},\]

with u = E/I. Σ(0) = 0, and Σ(U = 1) is the Bethe limit of the shell. That limit is not fitted: Dere computes it from the photoionization cross sections of Verner and Yakovlev (1995) by Younger's formula, his eq. (1), and notes that measured cross sections "have often been found to approach a high-energy limit that is a factor of 1.4 to 2.0 higher than the Bethe limit" (his Sect. 2.1). The spline is the cubic interpolating spline with not-a-knot ends, SciPy's splrep(s = 0), with which the fits were made (the comment of ChiantiPy's Ion.diCross, the CHIANTI package for Python; fiasco interpolates the same nodes with a monotone cubic, PchipInterpolator). curvatures holds its second derivatives at the nodes.
