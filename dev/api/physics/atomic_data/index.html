Runes2P.float_matrix — Method
float_matrix(rows) -> Matrix{Float64}

The TOML array of rows rows as a Matrix{Float64} with one row per element, so that a file reads like the Julia literal: [[1.0, 2.0, 3.0]] is the 1×3 matrix [1.0 2.0 3.0]. The elements are converted as by float_vector. It throws an error if the rows differ in length or if rows is empty.

The TOML array values as a Vector{Float64}. Floats are kept bit for bit (nan gives NaN); integers become the Float64 of the same value and throw an error if that value has no exact Float64. Any other element (a boolean, a string, an array) throws an error.

Runes2P.parse_data_file — Method
parse_data_file(path) -> Dict{String, Any}

The parsed content of the TOML data file at path, after a check of its header. It throws an error if the file has no table metadata, if one of DATA_FILE_METADATA_KEYS is missing there or is not a non-empty string, or if the table holds a value that is not a string: TOML puts every key after the header [metadata] into it, so the numbers go into a table of their own.

source
Runes2P.read_data_file — Method
read_data_file(name) -> Dict{String, Any}

The parsed content of the data file data/<name>.toml, its table metadata included, as parse_data_file gives it.

It is called in the body of the module, so the file is read once when the package is precompiled and the constants built from it cost nothing at run time. The file is registered with include_dependency by its content, so a change of the content recompiles the package and a copy of the depot elsewhere does not.

The arrays of the result are converted with float_vector, float_matrix and int_vector.

Runes2P.ADF11Table — Type
ADF11Table

Rate coefficients of one ADF11 file of OpenADAS for the element with nuclear charge nuclear_charge, read by read_adf11:

  • class: :acd (recombination), :scd (ionization), :ccd (charge exchange), all in m³/s, :plt (line radiation) and :prb (recombination and bremsstrahlung radiation), in W m³;
  • log_density [log₁₀ m⁻³] and log_temperature [log₁₀ eV]: the nodes;
  • log_coefficient[i, j, b]: log₁₀ of the coefficient at the density node i, the temperature node j, in block b of the file (one block per charge state);
  • derivatives: the derivatives at the nodes that DREAM's bicubic interpolation uses, with respect to the log density, the log temperature, and both;
  • source: the file.

adas_coefficient evaluates a coefficient.

source
Runes2P.adas_cache_directory — Method
adas_cache_directory()

The directory of the ADF11 files: ENV["RUNES2P_ADAS_CACHE"], or ~/.cache/Runes2P/adas. It is filled by scripts/fetch_adas.py on a machine with internet access (the compute nodes of clusters often have none) and is never part of the repository.

source
Runes2P.adas_coefficient — Method
adas_coefficient(table, Z0, free_electron_density, temperature; extrapolation=:dream)

The coefficient of table (ADF11Table) for the ion of charge Z0 at the free-electron density free_electron_density (n) [m⁻³] and the temperature temperature (T) [eV], in m³/s (W m³ for :plt and :prb). Zero where the class has no such charge state (recombination of the neutral, ionization of the bare nucleus) and at n ≤ 0 or T ≤ 0, as in DREAM.

The coefficients are those of ADAS (H. P. Summers, The ADAS User Manual, version 2.6 (2004), http://www.adas.ac.uk, with the data of OpenADAS, https://open.adas.ac.uk). The interpolation is DREAM's (src/ADASRateInterpolator.cpp): GSL's bicubic interpolation of log₁₀ of the coefficient in log₁₀ n and log₁₀ T inside the table, and outside it the bilinear interpolation of the edge cell, continued linearly (extrapolation = :dream). extrapolation = :clamp_density takes the density at the nearest edge of the table and extrapolates in the temperature alone: at low densities the coefficients tend to their coronal limit, which does not depend on the density. The default is :dream (a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package).

Derivatives with respect to n and T are exact through ForwardDiff, in place of DREAM's one-sided differences.

Runes2P.read_adf11 — Method
read_adf11(path; class=nothing)

Read an ADF11 file of OpenADAS (unresolved, one block per charge state) into an ADF11Table, in SI units: the densities from cm⁻³ to m⁻³ and the coefficients from cm³ to m³ (log₁₀ + 6 and − 6, as DREAM's tools/ADAS/data.py). class is taken from the file name (acd96_ne.dat is :acd) unless given.

Throws an ArgumentError for a file whose blocks do not match its header, and for a metastable-resolved file (IGRD or IPRT above 1), which DREAM does not read either.

Runes2P.ion_mass — Method
ion_mass(species; table=:standard)

The mass [kg] of an ion species. For nuclear charge 1 it is the mass of hydrogen, deuterium (the default, as in DREAM) or tritium by the name "H", "D" or "T"; a species of charge 1 with another name takes the mass of deuterium.

Runes2P.KINETIC_IONIZATION_FITS — Constant
KINETIC_IONIZATION_FITS

The parameters of the cross section of each element, one row per charge state Z0 = 0, …, Z − 1: the prefactor C, the effective ionization potential I [eV] and the near-threshold factor β* of ionization_cross_section. DREAM's fits to the ADAS SCD coefficients at zero density, copied from DREAM's src/Atomics/kineticionizationdata.cpp, generated by its tools/fit_kineticionization.py (MIT License, Copyright (c) 2020 Mathias Hoppe and Ola Embreus). DREAM gives their error as at most around tens of %. Against the ADAS SCD at the lowest density of the tables, the Maxwellian average at T = I lies between 1.03 and 1.57 of it for the states of argon and between 0.93 and 1.24 for those of neon (test/test_kinetic_ionization.jl). The values are read from the data file data/kinetic_ionization_fits.toml.

Runes2P.SECONDARY_SPECTRUM_WIDTHS — Constant
SECONDARY_SPECTRUM_WIDTHS

The width Ē [eV] of the energy spectrum of the secondary electrons of electron-impact ionization, dσ/dE_s ∝ 1/(1 + (E_s/Ē)²), per element, from Table II of C. B. Opal, W. K. Peterson and E. C. Beaty, J. Chem. Phys. 55, 4100 (1971), doi:10.1063/1.1676707, measured on neutral gases with primaries of 500 eV. Their fit, eq. (2), has the exponent 2.1; the exponent 2 used here is the one they allow for the integral of the spectrum (p. 4105). With it the mean over every secondary grows as ln E (argon at 1 MeV: 68.9 eV, against 44.3 eV with 2.1). The widths: helium 15.8, neon 24.2, argon 10.0, krypton 9.6 and xenon 8.7 eV; hydrogen, deuterium and tritium take the 8.3 eV of H₂. The authors call their fits to argon, krypton and xenon poor, so that "the values of Ē are not very significant for these gases"; Ē varies by about 15 % over primary energies of 100 to 2000 eV (their Table I). The widths were measured on neutral atoms and molecules with ionization energies of 9.5 to 24.6 eV and are applied to every charge state. They give no law for ions: the authors find Ē "poorly correlated with the ionization potential" (p. 4106). The default spectrum of the heating by secondaries and of the friction of bound_friction = :ionization_split is the binary-encounter-Bethe spectrum (beb_mean_secondary_energy; a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package), and these widths serve spectrum = :opal. Other elements take their width from the widths of SecondaryHeatingTerm and the secondary_widths of CollisionSettings.

Runes2P.beb_mean_secondary_energy — Method
beb_mean_secondary_energy(E, B, cut)

The mean kinetic energy [eV] of the secondary electron of an ionization of a subshell with the binding energy B by an electron of kinetic energy E, the energies and cut in eV, with the singly differential cross section of the binary-encounter-Bethe (BEB) model of Y.-K. Kim and M. E. Rudd, Phys. Rev. A 50, 3954 (1994), doi:10.1103/PhysRevA.50.3954, eqs. (52) to (54):

\[\frac{dσ}{dW} ∝ \sum_{n=1}^{3} F_n(t) \left[\frac{1}{(w + 1)^n} + \frac{1}{(t − w)^n}\right], \qquad F_1 = −\frac{F_2}{t + 1}, \quad F_2 = 2 − Q, \quad F_3 = Q \ln t,\]

with t = E/B and w = W/B, up to the smaller of (t − 1)/2 and cut/B, conditional on lying there, in closed form. The common factor of the F_n (1/(t + u + 1), or 1/(t + 1) for ions with open-shell valence electrons, their Sec. VI) cancels in the mean, so the spectrum needs only B. Q = 1, their approximation where the dipole oscillator strength is not known (Sec. IV), also for the one-electron ions, for which their eq. (60) gives Q = 0.5668: there the dipole term, which lies at small w, is too large, and the mean too low. Zero below the threshold. Where the upper limit, the smaller of (E − B)/2 and cut, lies below 1e-6 B, the closed form loses its digits, and the mean is that of the flat spectrum there, half that limit. The mean of a charge state takes the valence shell alone, with B its ionization energy; inner shells, which Kim and Rudd sum over, are left out. Normalized by the same integral, the spectrum gives their total cross section of the BEB model, (ln t/2)(1 − 1/t²) + 1 − 1/t − ln t/(t + 1) at Q = 1, which the tests check.

source
Runes2P.beb_secondary_energy_below — Method
beb_secondary_energy_below(E, B, cut)

The kinetic energy [eV] per ionization that the secondary electrons below cut carry, for an ionization of a subshell with the binding energy B by an electron of kinetic energy E, the energies and cut in eV, with the spectrum of beb_mean_secondary_energy. It is the partial moment of the spectrum up to w_<, the smaller of w_top and cut/B, divided by the count of every secondary up to w_top = (t − 1)/2, with t = E/B and w = W/B:

\[B\, \frac{\int_0^{w_{<}} w\, \frac{dσ}{dw}\, dw}{\int_0^{w_\mathrm{top}} \frac{dσ}{dw}\, dw}.\]

Unlike beb_mean_secondary_energy, the secondaries above cut count with zero energy instead of being left out. It is zero at cut = 0 and below the threshold. For cut ≥ B w_top it is the mean over every secondary, which beb_mean_secondary_energy(E, B, Inf) gives as well, except where w_top < 1e-6 and that function takes the flat spectrum. SecondaryHeatingTerm with cut = :knockon and spectrum = :beb gives this energy to the cold electrons.

Each integral is in closed form where its upper limit, w_< or w_top, is 0.03 or more. Below that limit the closed form loses digits, and the integral takes the Taylor series of the spectrum at w = 0 up to w^8. The two forms agree best at that switch.

Runes2P.ionization_cross_section — Method
ionization_cross_section(element, Z0, p; form=:chianti)

The cross section [m²] of the ionization of the ion of element in charge state Z0 by an electron of momentum p (in units of mₑc), DREAM's EvaluateBCGSingleSubshell (src/Equations/Fluid/IonKineticIonizationTerm.cpp): the formula of A. Burgess and M. C. Chidichimo, Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc. 203, 1269 (1983), doi:10.1093/mnras/203.4.1269,

\[σ_{BC} = C π a_0^2 \left(\frac{R_y}{I}\right)^2 \frac{\ln(U)^{1 + β^*/U}}{U}\]

with U = E/I and the kinetic energy E, blended into DREAM's form of the relativistic Bethe formula of N. A. Garland et al., Phys. Plasmas 27, 040702 (2020), doi:10.1063/5.0003638, C π a₀² α² (R_y/I) (ln(p²/2I) − β²) with I in units of mₑc² (IonKineticIonizationTerm.cpp:221-223), by the weight S = 1/(1 + exp(1 − E/100 keV)) of DREAM's code (:224). That form lacks the factor 1/β² of a Bethe cross section: it is β² times the continuation that joins the large-U limit of σ_{BC}, so it is about 1e-4 of σ_{BC} at thermal energies, while S gives it the weight 0.27 already at E = 0. The fitted C absorbs that weight at the energies of the fit, and the shape at hot-tail and runaway energies differs from a consistent blend. Zero below the threshold U < 1 and for the bare nucleus. The parameters are those of KINETIC_IONIZATION_FITS.

form selects the cross section:

  • :chianti (the default, a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package): the parameters of CHIANTI, continued relativistically by the Bethe theory (chianti_cross_section, CHIANTI_IONIZATION_FITS), for hydrogen, its isotopes (with the parameters of hydrogen), neon and argon. The other elements, tungsten among them, keep DREAM's form below: the table holds no CHIANTI data for them.
  • :dream: DREAM's form above, with DREAM's fits. From 0.1 to 1 keV it lies within 7 % of the measurements of hydrogen (Shah et al. 1987), and at 0.5 to 1 keV at 0.75 of the single ionization of argon (Straub et al. 1995); at 1 MeV it is 1.72 times the relativistic Bethe cross section of hydrogen and grows with the energy.
  • :bethe: the term divided by β², C π a₀² α² (R_y/I)(ln(p²/2I) − β²)/β², which joins the large-U limit of σ_{BC}, with the parameters KINETIC_IONIZATION_BETHE_FITS refitted by DREAM's method. The blend is floored at zero: just above the threshold of a state with I of several keV the logarithm ln(p²/2I) lies below β² while S is already 0.3 at 15 keV, so the blend would be negative there (W⁶⁴⁺, I = 15.6 keV).

Runes2P.mean_secondary_energy — Method
mean_secondary_energy(width, E, I, cut)

The mean kinetic energy [eV] of the secondary electron of an ionization with the ionization potential I by an electron of kinetic energy E, with the spectrum of Opal et al. of width width (SECONDARY_SPECTRUM_WIDTHS) up to the smaller of (E − I)/2 (the slower of the two electrons is the secondary) and cut. All four are in eV. The mean is (Ē/2) ln(1 + (E_top/Ē)²) / arctan(E_top/Ē). Zero below the threshold. This is the mean of the secondaries below the top, conditional on lying there. With cut = Inf it is the mean over every secondary, which SecondaryHeatingTerm with spectrum = :opal gives to the cold electrons and which the friction of bound_friction = :ionization_split with that spectrum takes from f (CollisionSettings): every secondary joins the cold electrons through the closure of their density, so both take the same moment.

source
Runes2P.secondary_energy_below — Method
secondary_energy_below(width, E, I, cut)

The kinetic energy [eV] per ionization that the secondary electrons below cut carry, for an ionization with the ionization potential I by an electron of kinetic energy E, with the spectrum of Opal et al. of width width (SECONDARY_SPECTRUM_WIDTHS). All four are in eV. It is the partial moment of the spectrum up to E_<, the smaller of E_top and cut, divided by the count of every secondary up to E_top = (E − I)/2, with Ē = width:

\[\frac{\int_0^{E_{<}} E_s\, S(E_s)\, dE_s}{\int_0^{E_\mathrm{top}} S(E_s)\, dE_s} = \frac{(Ē/2) \ln(1 + (E_{<}/Ē)^2)}{\arctan(E_\mathrm{top}/Ē)}, \qquad S(E_s) = \frac{1}{1 + (E_s/Ē)^2}.\]

Unlike mean_secondary_energy, the secondaries above cut count with zero energy instead of being left out. It is zero at cut = 0 and below the threshold, and equal to mean_secondary_energy(width, E, I, Inf) for cut ≥ E_top. SecondaryHeatingTerm with cut = :knockon and spectrum = :opal gives this energy to the cold electrons.

Runes2P.KINETIC_IONIZATION_BETHE_FITS — Constant
KINETIC_IONIZATION_BETHE_FITS

The parameters C, I [eV] and β* of ionization_cross_section with form = :bethe, one row per charge state Z0 = 0, …, Z − 1, as KINETIC_IONIZATION_FITS for DREAM's form. They are DREAM's fit run unchanged (tools/fit_kineticionization.py with tools/ADAS/fit.py, method "single", MIT License, Copyright (c) 2020 Mathias Hoppe and Ola Embreus) on the cross section with the factor 1/β²: per charge state, a least-squares fit of the logarithm of the Maxwell-Jüttner average of v σ to the ADAS SCD coefficient at the lowest density over the temperature window of DREAM's tools/kineticCrossSectionParams.json (2 to 100 eV where it has none), with C and β* free in [1e-4, 200] and [0, 1] and I the NIST ionization energy of DREAM's table. The constants are those of SciPy's scipy.constants in the pixi environment of reference/dream (CODATA 2022 from SciPy 1.15 on, 1e-9 from CODATA 2018), where DREAM's tool takes a₀ = 5.29e-11 m, R_y = 13.6 eV and α = 1/137; with DREAM's constants and form the same script reproduces KINETIC_IONIZATION_FITS (D: C = 3.05406, β* = 0.296804). Fitted by scripts/fit_kinetic_ionization.py on 2026-10-12 from the ADAS files of DREAM's cache (tools/cache). The values are in data/kinetic_ionization_bethe_fits.toml, which the script writes: in [data], one array per element with one row [C, I, β*] per charge state.

Against the SCD coefficient, the Maxwellian average lies between 0.71 and 1.15 at the two ends and the middle of the window of each state of argon (DREAM's form: 0.64 to 1.13). C is 0.72 to 0.74 of DREAM's for hydrogen and the states of argon up to Ar⁵⁺, near the weight 1 − S(0) = 0.73 of the Burgess-Chidichimo term at thermal energies that DREAM's C absorbs. β* reaches its upper bound 1 for Ne⁰ to Ne²⁺, Ar⁰ and Ar⁷⁺, as some of DREAM's fits approach it.

ChiantiIonization(ion, direct, autoionization_scale, autoionization)

The CHIANTI parameters of the ionization of one ion (ion, CHIANTI's name, "ar_4" for Ar IV, the charge state 3): the shells of its direct ionization (ChiantiDirectShell), and its excitation-autoionization (ChiantiAutoionization) with the scale of the last line of its .diparams file (1 for every ion of the table). Runes2P.chianti_cross_section — Method
chianti_cross_section(fit::ChiantiIonization, p)

The cross section [m²] of the ionization of the ion of fit by an electron of momentum p (in units of mₑc): CHIANTI's direct ionization and excitation-autoionization (K. P. Dere, Astron. Astrophys. 466, 771 (2007), doi:10.1051/0004-6361:20066728), continued relativistically by the Bethe theory.

The relativistic Bethe cross section of M. Inokuti, Rev. Mod. Phys. 43, 297 (1971), doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.43.297, eq. (4.26), for an electron (z = 1),

\[σ_B = \frac{4π a_0^2 R_y}{T} \left\{M^2 \left[\ln\frac{β^2}{1 - β^2} - β^2\right] + C\right\}, \qquad T = \frac{m_e v^2}{2},\]

splits, by Fano's analysis (Inokuti, p. 328), into the nonrelativistic Bethe form (his eq. (4.18)) at the energy T = mₑv²/2 and the relativistic rise (4π a₀² R_y/T) M² [−ln(1 − β²) − β²]. The fits of CHIANTI are nonrelativistic: their high-energy limit is the nonrelativistic Bethe limit (Dere, Sect. 2.1). They are continued in the same way:

  • each shell and each transition of the autoionization is evaluated at the speed of the electron, u = β²/β_I² with β_I the speed of an electron of the threshold energy, which is E/I at low energies and keeps the threshold at E = I;
  • the relativistic rise is added with the coefficient M² of the high-energy limit of each shell and allowed transition (ChiantiIonization), weighted by 1 − 1/u so that it starts at zero at the threshold of each (a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package).

Above CHIANTI_MATCHING_ENERGY the cross section is σ_B with the M² of the fit and the C at which σ_B equals the continued fit at that energy, joined to it by the weight of CHIANTI_MATCHING_WIDTH. Below about 10 keV the continuation changes the fit by less than the spread of the data: by 0.3 % at 1 keV and about 3 % at 10 keV for the neutral atoms.

Implied by the fits: M² = 0.2842 for H (0.2823 from the fit of M. B. Shah, D. S. Elliott, H. B. Gilbody, J. Phys. B 20, 3501 (1987)), 4.180 for Ar (4.22, F. F. Rieke, W. Prepejchal, Phys. Rev. A 6, 1507 (1972), Table II) and 1.705 for Ne (2.02, the same table, 16 % low, within Dere's caveat of the Bethe limit, ChiantiDirectShell); C = 3.92 for H (4.007) and 40.8 for Ar (37.93).

The threshold is CHIANTI's ionization energy, which differs from that of ionization_energy (NIST) by up to 1.4 eV for the argon ions; the energy loss of the friction of bound_friction = :ionization_split takes NIST's. Generic in the number type of p.

Runes2P.CHIANTI_MATCHING_WIDTH — Constant
CHIANTI_MATCHING_WIDTH

The factor, 2, by which the weight of chianti_cross_section passes from the continued fit to the Bethe form, from CHIANTI_MATCHING_ENERGY/2 to 2 CHIANTI_MATCHING_ENERGY, with the cubic w = x²(3 − 2x) in x = ln(2E/E_m)/ln 4. The weight and its slope are continuous at both ends, so the cross section and its slope are continuous. The factor is a choice of this package (see the docs page Choices of this package).

Runes2P.ChiantiAutoionization — Type
ChiantiAutoionization(kind, oscillator_strength, energy, scale, values)

One transition of the excitation-autoionization of an ion in CHIANTI's .easplom files (K. P. Dere, Astron. Astrophys. 466, 771 (2007), Sect. 2.2): the Burgess-Tully type of the transition (kind), its weighted oscillator strength, its excitation energy [Ry, the unit of the files] (energy), the Burgess-Tully parameter C (scale) and the five spline nodes of the scaled collision strength at equally spaced scaled energies from 0 to 1 (values). With X = E/ΔE, the collision strength Ω is, by type, as ChiantiPy's util.splomDescale evaluates it:

  • type 1: Ω = Ω_s(1 − ln C/ln(X − 1 + C)) ln(X − 1 + e);
  • type 2: Ω = Ω_s((X − 1)/(X − 1 + C));
  • type 3: Ω = Ω_s((X − 1)/(X − 1 + C))/X²;
  • type 4: Ω = Ω_s(1 − ln C/ln(X − 1 + C)) ln(X − 1 + C).

The cross section is s π a₀² Ω/(E/R_y) with the scale s of the ion (ChiantiIonization); the statistical weight of the ground level is in Ω (ChiantiPy's Ion.eaCross). Types 1 and 4, the allowed transitions, grow as ln X and add s Ω_s(1)/4 to the Bethe coefficient M² of the ion.

Runes2P.ChiantiDirectShell — Type
ChiantiDirectShell(scale, threshold, knots, values)

The fit of the direct ionization of one shell of an ion in CHIANTI's .diparams files (K. P. Dere, Astron. Astrophys. 466, 771 (2007), doi:10.1051/0004-6361:20066728, Sect. 2.1): the Burgess-Tully parameter f (scale), the ionization energy of the shell [eV] (threshold), and the spline nodes of the scaled cross section Σ (values, in 1e-14 cm² eV², the unit of the files) at the scaled energies U (knots, from 0 to 1). Dere's eqs. (2) and (3):

\[U = 1 - \frac{\ln f}{\ln(u - 1 + f)}, \qquad Σ = \frac{u σ I^2}{\ln u + 1},\]

with u = E/I. Σ(0) = 0, and Σ(U = 1) is the Bethe limit of the shell. That limit is not fitted: Dere computes it from the photoionization cross sections of Verner and Yakovlev (1995) by Younger's formula, his eq. (1), and notes that measured cross sections "have often been found to approach a high-energy limit that is a factor of 1.4 to 2.0 higher than the Bethe limit" (his Sect. 2.1). The spline is the cubic interpolating spline with not-a-knot ends, SciPy's splrep(s = 0), with which the fits were made (the comment of ChiantiPy's Ion.diCross, the CHIANTI package for Python; fiasco interpolates the same nodes with a monotone cubic, PchipInterpolator). curvatures holds its second derivatives at the nodes.

Runes2P.CHIANTI_IONIZATION_FITS — Constant
CHIANTI_IONIZATION_FITS

The parameters of the cross sections of the ionization of each element, one ChiantiIonization per charge state Z0 = 0, …, Z − 1: the direct ionization and the excitation-autoionization of the CHIANTI database, version 11.0.2 (the VERSION file of its SolarSoft mirror, https://soho.nascom.nasa.gov/solarsoft/packages/chianti/dbase/VERSION, read on 2026-10-13), from its files <ion>.diparams and <ion>.easplom, unchanged since K. P. Dere, Astron. Astrophys. 466, 771 (2007), doi:10.1051/0004-6361:20066728. Dere fits measured cross sections where they exist, mostly for the neutral and singly charged ions, and his distorted-wave calculations with the Flexible Atomic Code otherwise; the final errors of his Tables 1 to 28 have the median 10 % and the mean 13 % (his Sect. 3.31). The sources of the ions of the table, from his Sect. 3:

  • H I: the measurements of Shah et al. (1987), error 7 %. Deuterium and tritium take the parameters of hydrogen.
  • Ne I, Ar I and Ar II among others: fits to measurements (Ar I to Straub et al. 1995 and five other sets).
  • Ne IX, Ne X, Ar XVII, Ar XVIII (the one- and two-electron ions): Dere's text takes the relativistic distorted-wave fits of Fontes et al. (1999) for these, which ChiantiPy evaluates instead of the files; the table holds the parameters of the files, as fiasco evaluates them. Against the relativistic distorted-wave calculations of R. Bao and B. Li (At. Data Nucl. Data Tables 159, 101663 (2024)), the files of Ar¹⁶⁺ and Ar¹⁷⁺ give 1.24 times their cross section at 5 times the threshold and 1.35 and 1.38 times it at 10 times.
  • the other ions: the calculations, with the excitation-autoionization of Ne VII, Ne VIII, Ar III to Ar VIII, Ar XV and Ar XVI.

The parameters are in the data file data/chianti_ionization.toml, which scripts/generate_chianti_ionization.jl writes from the files of CHIANTI.
