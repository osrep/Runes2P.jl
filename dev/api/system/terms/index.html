Runes2P.EquationTerm — Type
EquationTerm

Supertype of the terms of an Equation. A term implements

  • dependencies(term): names of the unknowns it reads;
  • coupling(term): :sparse (its Jacobian block is stored; sparsity! lists the structural nonzeros) or :matrix_free (applied through jacobian_vector_product!, e.g. the knock-on term, whose block is dense per radius);
  • rhs!(out, term, u, layout, row, t): add the term to out in the rows of the unknown row, as a pure function of the state u and the time t [s]. :sparse terms must accept state vectors of dual numbers, since their Jacobian comes from AD;
  • sparsity!(entries, term, layout, row) for :sparse terms: push (row index, column index) pairs of the structural nonzeros;
  • jacobian_vector_product!(out, term, u, v, layout, row, t) for :matrix_free terms: add (∂ rhs_term / ∂u) v to out;

A :sparse term whose row holds many unknowns (a moment of a distribution, the loss through the edge) declares jacobian_assembly(term) === :direct and adds its Jacobian entries itself with add_jacobian!, so that the coloring of the AD Jacobian does not see its rows.

A term may also define treatment(term) (:implicit by default; :explicit terms are evaluated at the previous time level and contribute nothing to the Jacobian), prepare_step! (a part of an implicit term lagged to the previous time level), validate(term, layout, row) (checks against the layout, run when the system is built), is_linear(term) (false by default: whether the term is linear in the state, up to a constant, which lets a linear system accept a Newton step on the update test alone) and, for :sparse terms whose stored block is an approximation of their Jacobian (a quasi-Newton matrix), jacobian_kind(term) === :approximate with jacobian_correction_product! supplying the difference to the exact product.

A term defined outside the package adds its methods under the qualified names (Runes2P.dependencies(term::MyTerm) = ..., function Runes2P.rhs!(out, term::MyTerm, ...)). The functions that callers use are exported. validate, whose name is common to many packages, add_local_jacobian!, lags_in_ode_form and the plasma columns of a term (has_plasma_columns, plasma_column_sparsity!, add_plasma_columns!), which a term implements and the system calls, are declared public and not exported.

add_jacobian!(J, term, u, layout, row, t)

Runes2P.add_local_jacobian! — Method
add_local_jacobian!(J, term, u, layout, row, t)

Add the Jacobian of a term whose rows at a radius read only the radial unknowns of that radius (its dependencies) to J at the state u and the time t [s], by one evaluation of its rhs! with dual numbers: partial k is seeded on the k-th component of the dependencies at every radius at once, so that the partials of a row at radius ir are its derivatives with respect to the unknowns at ir. A term with a row that reads all charge states of a radius (the radiated power, the conductivity, the energy exchange with the ions) assembles its block this way, so that the coloring of the automatic Jacobian does not need a color per charge state. The tag is fixed, so the code compiles once per number of local columns.

The evaluation runs on a state of the dependencies and the row alone, in a layout of these radial unknowns on the same radial grid (local_layout), so that its cost does not grow with the kinetic unknowns of the system.

Runes2P.jacobian_assembly — Method
jacobian_assembly(term)

How the stored Jacobian block of a :sparse term is filled: :automatic (default) by colored forward-mode AD of rhs!, or :direct by the term's own add_jacobian!.

The colored AD needs one pass of the sparse terms per color, and two columns that share a row need different colors. A row that holds every cell of a radius (the density or current moment of a distribution, the loss through the edge) would therefore take one color per cell. Such terms assemble their block directly, and the coloring sees the other terms alone. A direct term is tested against the AD of its rhs!.

source
Runes2P.jacobian_correction_product! — Method
jacobian_correction_product!(out, term, u, v, layout, row, t)

For a :sparse term with jacobian_kind(term) === :approximate: add (J_exact - J_stored) v to out, the difference between the exact Jacobian-vector product of the term's residual and the product of its stored block, both at the state u and the time t [s]. Nothing for exact terms.

source
Runes2P.jacobian_kind — Method
jacobian_kind(term)

:exact (default) when the stored Jacobian block of a :sparse term is the derivative of its residual, :approximate when it is a cheaper matrix (a frozen limiter, another scheme's Jacobian, FokkerPlanckTerm). With an approximate block the Newton iteration on the stored matrix converges linearly (a quasi-Newton method), which NewtonSolver accounts for; GMRES(operator=:exact) restores the exact Newton operator through jacobian_correction_product!.

Runes2P.prepare_step! — Method
prepare_step!(term, u_old, layout, row, t)

Called with the state at the start of the step and the time t [s] that ends the step, before every evaluation of the residual (residual!), and with the state itself and its time in the ODE form (rhs!). fill_initial_values! calls it for the terms of the rows it fills, with the state and the time it is given, before the fill. The construction of an EquationSystem calls it with the initial state for a KineticEnergyLossTerm of a source with lagged = :all that was never prepared. A term with a part lagged to the previous time level inside an implicit term (the knock-on term with treatment = :explicit_primary) evaluates that part here. Nothing by default.

A term with a lagged part, evaluated on its own outside a system, takes that part from the state of its last preparation, whatever state is passed. Before any preparation, the knock-on term with a lagged primary and the KineticEnergyLossTerm of a source with lagged = :all throw an ArgumentError; a FokkerPlanckTerm with lagged = :all and a FieldParticleTerm with a plasma of unknowns keep the coefficients of the operator they were built with, and a Fokker-Planck term with lagged ions takes the ions of the state.

Runes2P.LinearTerm — Type
LinearTerm(unknown; coefficient=1)

coefficient times the unknown unknown, added entry by entry to the row unknown, which must have the same kind and sizes (e.g. -1 times an unknown in its own algebraic equation, or a decay rate). coefficient is a number, or one number per radius for a radial or kinetic unknown: a conductivity in Ohm's law 0 = σ E − j. The vector is referenced, so an update of the run may change its values between the intervals. The term takes the unit of its row, so coefficient has the unit of the row over that of unknown.

source
Runes2P.PrescribedValueTerm — Type
PrescribedValueTerm(values)

A prescribed value added to the row of a radial unknown with one component or of a scalar unknown: a number, one number per radius, or a function of the time [s] that returns either. The values take the unit of the row. For a radial unknown the function may also return a callable of r, such as an interpolator: it is evaluated on the cell centres once per time, and a profile on points of its own that is constant in time is t -> profile. With LinearTerm(name; coefficient = -1) in an algebraic equation it holds the unknown at the prescribed value, 0 = value(t) - unknown, which makes a prescribed profile an unknown of the system: a prescribed electric field E(r, t) that the kinetic terms read from the state.

Runes2P.CurrentMomentTerm — Type
CurrentMomentTerm(kinetic, grid; coefficient=1, pitch_rule=:linear, region=nothing)

coefficient times the current density [A/m² for f in m⁻³] of the kinetic unknown kinetic on grid (current_density; pitch_rule = :cell_center is DREAM's j_hot moment), added to a radial row unknown with one component (the ohmic or hot-electron current defined by an algebraic equation). The pitch weights are built per radius when the term is created, so different momentum grids at different radii work; grid must be the unknown's grid (the same radii and momentum faces, checked when the system is built).

With region, a MomentumRegion, the moment covers that part of the momentum grid, with the limit of the region at the time of the evaluation: the current of the hot electrons above the threshold of the hot region.

The term keeps the positions of its row in the stored Jacobian between calls, so its add_jacobian! is not safe to call concurrently.

Runes2P.DensityMomentTerm — Type
DensityMomentTerm(kinetic; coefficient=1)
DensityMomentTerm(kinetic, grid; coefficient=1, region=nothing)

coefficient times the density n(r) = (1/V') Σ 𝒱' Δp Δξ f [m⁻³ for f in m⁻³] of the kinetic unknown kinetic, added to a radial row unknown with one component (e.g. a hot-electron density defined by an algebraic equation). Works with different momentum grids at different radii.

With region, a MomentumRegion, the moment covers that part of the momentum grid: the hot electrons above the threshold of the hot region. The limit of the region at the time of the evaluation counts, and grid is the grid of the unknown. A prescribed limit makes the term linear in the distribution. A limit that follows the temperature unknown (ThermalMomentum with the unknown's name) makes it nonlinear, and its Jacobian holds the column of the temperature through the threshold.

The term keeps the positions of its row in the stored Jacobian between calls, so its add_jacobian! is not safe to call concurrently.

Runes2P.FokkerPlanckTerm — Type
FokkerPlanckTerm(unknown, operator::AdvectionDiffusion; jacobian=:exact, electric_field=nothing,
                 plasma=nothing, settings=nothing, synchrotron=nothing, lagged=:ions)

Advection-diffusion (Fokker-Planck) operator applied to the kinetic unknown unknown, added to the equation of the row unknown (normally the same). Stored sparse, with the 3×3 (p, ξ) stencil and the second neighbours of the four-point schemes. The corners of the 3×3 block are stored when the operator declares cross diffusion, and the radial neighbours when it declares radial transport: the cell of the same pitch at each neighbouring radius, or with different pitch grids the cells that overlap it (stencil_pattern). Needs the same cell counts and p faces at all radii.

electric_field names the radial unknown of the electric field [V/m] when the field is an unknown of the system. The advection of every face is then that of the operator, which holds the collisions, plus the field of the radius times the coefficients of a unit field (set_unit_field_coefficients!), inside the scheme. The operator must not hold the field of a prescribed plasma as well (fokker_planck_operator(...; field_in_coefficients = false)). The term is bilinear in the distribution and the field, and its stored block holds the column of the field of each radius, which is DREAM's Jacobian of its ElectricFieldTerm.

plasma is the source of the plasma that the collision coefficients follow: a PlasmaSource, a PlasmaState or a function of time that returns one (PrescribedPlasma). The coefficients of operator are rebuilt from it with settings and synchrotron (set_fokker_planck_coefficients!), and the scheme and the boundary conditions of operator are kept. Without them the term takes those that built operator (fokker_planck_operator), and CollisionSettings() with the synchrotron loss for an operator built otherwise (on by default, a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package). The rebuild happens when the plasma values of any radius differ from those of the last rebuild (update_key!), not at every new time, and rebuilds all radii. Without plasma the coefficients of operator are fixed. prescribed_fokker_planck_term builds such a term from a function of time. The rebuild writes no radial transport and no cross diffusion, so a term with plasma clears both declarations of operator (AdvectionDiffusion), and its stored block couples neither the radii nor the corners of the 3×3 block.

With a plasma that reads unknowns (PlasmaFromUnknowns) the term depends on them and is not linear. lagged names the part of that plasma taken at the start of the step; the rest is taken at the state of the Newton iterate, as DREAM does, and the stored Jacobian holds its columns (add_plasma_columns!) next to the block in f. With the ion densities lagged and the temperature and the density implicit, the stored Jacobian has the columns of the temperature and the density and none of the charge states, and the residual is consistent within the step. DREAM's disruption example drops these columns from its Jacobian alone (enableIonJacobian(False), examples/Disruptions/generate.py:243).

  • lagged = :all: the whole plasma at the start of the step, set in prepare_step!, with the prescribed part of the plasma at the time of the evaluation. The term is linear in f within a step and its block is exact. In the ODE form (rhs! of the system, the SciML interface) prepare_step! sees the state itself, so the term is implicit there. A term evaluated on its own keeps the coefficients of its last preparation, or those of operator when it was never prepared.
  • lagged = :ions (the default, a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package): the ion densities of the plasma when it reads them, no group otherwise. A term that lags them is of first order under BDF2, and ode_function passes no Jacobian for a system with it (lags_in_ode_form). The kinetic ionization and a corrected conductivity read the charge states of the iterate, so within a step the ions enter the collisions of f and those terms at two states, a difference of first order in the step. A term evaluated on its own takes the ion densities of its last prepare_step!, or those of the state when it was never prepared.
  • lagged = (): nothing lagged; the columns of the charge states are part of the plasma columns.
  • lagged = (:ion_densities,): the ion densities by name, as :ions for that unknown.

jacobian selects what the stored Jacobian block holds for a flux-limiter scheme (DREAM's ad_jac setting of AdvectionInterpolation). The residual is always the operator's own, so every mode converges to the same solution:

  • :exact: the Jacobian of the residual by colored forward-mode AD, including the limiter's derivative ψ′ (DREAM AD_INTERP_JACOBIAN_FULL, which carries ψ′ only for the limiters DREAM counts as smooth);
  • :frozen_limiter: the limiter weights frozen at the iterate and differentiated as constants (apply!(...; limiter_state), DREAM AD_INTERP_JACOBIAN_LINEAR);
  • a linear scheme name (:upwind, :central, :exponential_fitting, :quick, :upwind_second_order): the Jacobian of the operator with that scheme in p and ξ (with_scheme; :upwind is DREAM AD_INTERP_JACOBIAN_UPWIND), with that scheme's stencil (3-point for the two-point schemes).

For a scheme linear in f the frozen limiter is the exact Jacobian, and another linear scheme's is still a different matrix (upwind's for a central residual, the approximation that rescues some DREAM runs at high momentum resolution with central interpolation; see the docs page Departures from DREAM). With an approximate block the term declares jacobian_kind(term) === :approximate: the Newton iteration on the stored matrix is a quasi-Newton one that converges linearly, or, with GMRES(operator=:exact), the stored block only preconditions the exact Newton operator (jacobian_correction_product!, one dual-number pass of the operator per product).

Runes2P.add_plasma_columns! — Method
add_plasma_columns!(J, term, u, layout, row, t)

Add the plasma columns of a term with has_plasma_columns to J at the state u and the time t [s]. A FokkerPlanckTerm builds its coefficients from the plasma with dual numbers in the temperature and the density of every radius (dual_plasma) and applies them to f: the partials are the derivatives of the residual through the scheme's own face weights, the Péclet number of exponential fitting included. The scheme is the operator's own, as is the residual, whatever jacobian selects for the block in f. The coefficients in dual numbers are rebuilt when the plasma values of any radius differ from those of their last rebuild, as the coefficients of the residual (refresh_coefficients!), so the terms that read them share one rebuild per state.

Runes2P.has_plasma_columns — Method
has_plasma_columns(term)

Whether the stored Jacobian holds columns of the plasma unknowns of a term: the derivative of the term with respect to the temperature and the density of each radius, which the colored AD of the term does not see, since its coefficients hold numbers. Such a term lists them with plasma_column_sparsity! and adds them with add_plasma_columns!. True for a FokkerPlanckTerm whose plasma reads unknowns and whose coefficients are implicit. The terms that read the coefficients of such a term (MomentumBoundaryFluxTerm, MomentumInterfaceTerm, InterfaceEnergyFluxTerm, InterfaceParticleFluxTerm, InterfaceCollisionWorkTerm) pass its plasma columns on.

Runes2P.lags_in_ode_form — Method
lags_in_ode_form(term)

Runes2P.KnockOnTerm — Type
KnockOnTerm(primary, operator::KnockOnOperator; target_density, treatment=:explicit_primary)

Knock-on source [m⁻³ s⁻¹], the rate of the distribution of the kinetic row unknown, produced by the kinetic unknown primary on that unknown (operator maps the primary grid to the row unknown's grid, per radius, see apply_at_radius!). target_density is the target (total electron) density [m⁻³]: the name of a radial unknown, or a vector with one value per radius. Works with different momentum grids at different radii.

treatment:

  • :explicit_primary (default, DREAM's Boltzmann operator MollerBoltzmannOperator): the primary distribution at the previous time level, the target density at the new one. The term is then the target density times a source fixed for the step, evaluated once per start state of a step (prepare_step!) and again after a change of the cutoff (set_cutoff!), and its Jacobian is the column of the target density at each radius. With a prescribed target density it is the same as :explicit.
  • :explicit: the whole term at the previous time level, target density included.
  • :implicit: the whole term at the new time level, matrix-free, since it couples all cells of a radius. Every Krylov iteration of GMRES applies the operator once, and the direct solvers (SparseLU, MKLPardiso) do not take it.

The explicit forms are the cheaper step; the implicit form is the more accurate one per step. The explicit default follows DREAM.

Runes2P.MomentumInterfaceTerm — Type
MomentumInterfaceTerm(lower, upper, lower_term, upper_operator;
                      pitch_mapping=:overlap, scheme=<scheme of the lower operator>)

Particle flux through the momentum face shared by the kinetic unknowns lower and upper: the last p face of the grid of lower (the hot-tail grid) is the first p face of the grid of upper (the runaway grid) at every radius. The pitch grids of the two may differ. The same term goes into both equations. In the equation of lower it removes the flux from the last momentum cells, in the equation of upper it adds the flux to the first momentum cells, and both are computed from the same numbers, so no particle is lost or created at the interface.

lower_term is the Fokker-Planck term of lower (FokkerPlanckTerm or prescribed_fokker_planck_term), or its operator. A prescribed term is passed as the term: a system refuses the bare operator of a prescribed term of the system, whose coefficients belong to the time of its last rebuild. The flux takes the coefficients Aᵖ and Dᵖᵖ of that operator at its last p face, as DREAM's PXiExternalKineticKinetic does, and the two operators add no flux of their own through the face: the lower one has p_upper_boundary = :interface, upper_operator has p_lower_boundary = :interface. When lower_term takes the electric field as an unknown, the advection at the face holds the field of the state, and the term depends on the field as well. The cross term Dᵖᵠ at the face is not included (none in DREAM either, and zero for the collision operator). When the coefficients of lower_term follow the plasma unknowns (has_plasma_columns), so does the flux: its columns of the temperature, the density, and the ion densities unless the lower term lags them, come from the flux of the term's coefficients in dual numbers (add_plasma_columns!), as DREAM's PXiExternalKineticKinetic adds the derivatives of its coefficients (AddPartialJacobianContributions).

pitch_mapping:

  • :overlap (Runes2P): one flux per overlap cell of the two pitch grids (PitchOverlap), from the value of the lower cell and the value of the upper cell the overlap cell lies in,

    Γ_s = flux(Aᵖ_j, Dᵖᵖ_j, f_lower[end, j], f_upper[1, J]; scheme),

    with Γ_s [m⁻³ s⁻¹] the momentum flux Γᵖ at the face, before its weight 𝒱', and flux the two-point flux of scheme between the two cell centres (:upwind, :central or :exponential_fitting, by default the p scheme of the lower operator, and :upwind when that is a four-point scheme). The couplings are those of the scheme between two neighbouring cells, so exponential fitting and upwind keep their sign properties across the interface.

  • :dream: the flux of DREAM, one per pitch cell of the lower grid. The upper value is interpolated linearly in the pitch to the centre of the lower cell, the advection is first-order upwind whatever the scheme of the interior, the diffusion is the difference of the two values over the distance of the centres, and the flux is given to the upper cells by their overlap with the lower cell. Ported as written, including the rule for a lower centre beyond the last upper centre, which takes the second-to-last upper cell (the nearest one at the other end). scheme does not apply. Where the pitch grids differ, the interpolation couples an upper cell to its neighbours in the pitch with negative weights in the diffusive part, so this mapping does not keep the sign of f by construction.

Both conserve particles to round-off. Linear in the state unless the coefficients follow the field or the plasma unknowns; stored sparse. interface_flux gives the flux through the face as a rate per volume.

Runes2P.interface_flux — Function
interface_flux(term::MomentumInterfaceTerm, u, layout, ir, t=0)

Particle flux per unit volume [m⁻³ s⁻¹] through the momentum interface at radius index ir, positive from the lower to the upper grid, for the state u at the time t [s]: the rate at which the upper grid gains particles from the lower one, in the normalization of density and momentum_flux. With the interface above the critical momentum this is the runaway rate.

Runes2P.LossRateTerm — Type
LossRateTerm(unknown, rate)

Prescribed loss -ν f of the kinetic unknown unknown with the rate ν [1/s] of each radius (rate, one value per radius), added to the row unknown, which has the same grid. The exponential loss of a region with open field lines, as DREAM's loss outside the last closed flux surface. The vector is referenced: an update of the run may change its values between the intervals. The system refuses the term in the equation of a distribution whose energy is in the total energy of the electrons (total_energy_equation), since no term carries the energy of the lost electrons.

Runes2P.RadialEdgeLossTerm — Type
RadialEdgeLossTerm(kinetic, operator; coefficient=1)

coefficient times the rate [m⁻¹ s⁻¹], the electrons per second per metre of the cylinder, divided by 2π, at which the kinetic unknown kinetic loses particles through the edge of the plasma by the radial transport of operator (radial_particle_flux at the last radial face), added to a scalar row unknown. With TimeDerivative() the row unknown is the content lost so far [m⁻¹], in the normalization of total_particles, integrated with the same time levels as the distribution, so that the content of the distribution plus the lost particles stays constant to round-off when nothing else changes the content. The term reads the coefficients of operator as they are, also when an update changes them during the run.
