Runes2P.EquationTerm — Type
EquationTerm

Supertype of the terms of an Equation. A term implements

  • dependencies(term): names of the unknowns it reads;
  • coupling(term): :sparse (its Jacobian block is stored; sparsity! lists the structural nonzeros) or :matrix_free (applied through jacobian_vector_product!, e.g. the knock-on term, whose block is dense per radius);
  • rhs!(out, term, u, layout, row, t): add the term to out in the rows of the unknown row, as a pure function of the state u and the time t. :sparse terms must accept state vectors of dual numbers, since their Jacobian comes from AD;
  • sparsity!(entries, term, layout, row) for :sparse terms: push (row index, column index) pairs of the structural nonzeros;
  • jacobian_vector_product!(out, term, u, v, layout, row, t) for :matrix_free terms: add (∂ rhs_term / ∂u) v to out;

A :sparse term whose row holds many unknowns (a moment of a distribution, the loss through the edge) declares jacobian_assembly(term) === :direct and adds its Jacobian entries itself with add_jacobian!, so that the coloring of the AD Jacobian does not see its rows.

A term may also define treatment(term) (:implicit by default; :explicit terms are evaluated at the previous time level and contribute nothing to the Jacobian), prepare_step! (a part of an implicit term lagged to the previous time level), validate(term, layout, row) (checks against the layout, run when the system is built), is_linear(term) (false by default: whether the term is linear in the state, up to a constant, which lets a linear system accept a Newton step on the update test alone) and, for :sparse terms whose stored block is an approximation of their Jacobian (a quasi-Newton matrix), jacobian_kind(term) === :approximate with jacobian_correction_product! supplying the difference to the exact product.

A term defined outside the package adds its methods under the qualified names (Runes2P.dependencies(term::MyTerm) = ..., function Runes2P.rhs!(out, term::MyTerm, ...)). validate is not exported, since only the construction of an EquationSystem calls it and the name is common to many packages; the other functions are exported for calls.

Runes2P.add_local_jacobian! — Method
add_local_jacobian!(J, term, u, layout, row, t)

Add the Jacobian of a term whose rows at a radius read only the radial unknowns of that radius (its dependencies) to J, by one evaluation of its rhs! with dual numbers: partial k is seeded on the k-th component of the dependencies at every radius at once, so that the partials of a row at radius ir are its derivatives with respect to the unknowns at ir. A term with a row that reads all charge states of a radius (the radiated power, the conductivity, the energy exchange with the ions) assembles its block this way, so that the coloring of the automatic Jacobian does not need a color per charge state. The tag is fixed, so the code compiles once per number of local columns.

The evaluation runs on a state of the dependencies and the row alone, in a layout of these radial unknowns on the same radial grid (local_layout), so that its cost does not grow with the kinetic unknowns of the system.

Runes2P.jacobian_assembly — Method
jacobian_assembly(term)

How the stored Jacobian block of a :sparse term is filled: :automatic (default) by colored forward-mode AD of rhs!, or :direct by the term's own add_jacobian!.

The colored AD needs one pass of the sparse terms per color, and two columns that share a row need different colors. A row that holds every cell of a radius (the density or current moment of a distribution, the loss through the edge) would therefore take one color per cell. Such terms assemble their block directly, and the coloring sees the other terms alone. A direct term is tested against the AD of its rhs!.

Runes2P.FokkerPlanckTerm — Type
FokkerPlanckTerm(unknown, operator::AdvectionDiffusion; jacobian=:exact, electric_field=nothing,
                 plasma=nothing, settings=nothing, synchrotron=nothing, lagged=:ions)

Advection-diffusion (Fokker-Planck) operator applied to the kinetic unknown unknown, added to the equation of the row unknown (normally the same). Stored sparse, with the 3×3 (p, ξ) stencil, the second neighbours of the four-point schemes and the radial neighbours: the cell of the same pitch at each neighbouring radius, or with different pitch grids the cells that overlap it. Needs the same cell counts and p faces at all radii.

electric_field names the radial unknown of the electric field [V/m] when the field is an unknown of the system. The advection of every face is then that of the operator, which holds the collisions, plus the field of the radius times the coefficients of a unit field (set_unit_field_coefficients!), inside the scheme. The operator must not hold the field of a prescribed plasma as well (fokker_planck_operator(...; electric_field = false)). The term is bilinear in the distribution and the field, and its stored block holds the column of the field of each radius, which is DREAM's Jacobian of its ElectricFieldTerm.

plasma is the source of the plasma that the collision coefficients follow: a PlasmaSource, a PlasmaState or a function of time that returns one (PrescribedPlasma). The coefficients of operator are rebuilt from it with settings and synchrotron (set_fokker_planck_coefficients!), and the scheme and the boundary conditions of operator are kept. Without them the term takes those that built operator (fokker_planck_operator), and CollisionSettings() with the synchrotron loss for an operator built otherwise (on by default, a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package). The rebuild happens when the plasma values of any radius differ from those of the last rebuild (update_key!), not at every new time, and rebuilds all radii. Without plasma the coefficients of operator are fixed. PrescribedFokkerPlanckTerm builds such a term from a function of time.

With a plasma that reads unknowns (PlasmaFromUnknowns) the term depends on them and is not linear. lagged names the part of that plasma taken at the start of the step; the rest is taken at the state of the Newton iterate, as DREAM does, and the stored Jacobian holds its columns (add_plasma_columns!) next to the block in f. With the ion densities lagged and the temperature and the density implicit, the stored Jacobian has the columns of the temperature and the density and none of the charge states, and the residual is consistent within the step. DREAM's disruption example drops these columns from its Jacobian alone (enableIonJacobian(False), examples/Disruptions/generate.py:243).

  • lagged = :all: the whole plasma at the start of the step, set in prepare_step!, with the prescribed part of the plasma at the time of the evaluation. The term is linear in f within a step and its block is exact. In the ODE form (rhs! of the system, the SciML interface) prepare_step! sees the state itself, so the term is implicit there.
  • lagged = :ions (the default, a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package): the ion densities of the plasma when it reads them, no group otherwise. A term that lags them is of first order under BDF2, and ode_function passes no Jacobian for a system with it (lags_in_ode_form). The kinetic ionization and a corrected conductivity read the charge states of the iterate, so within a step the ions enter the collisions of f and those terms at two states, a difference of first order in the step.
  • lagged = (): nothing lagged; the columns of the charge states are part of the plasma columns.
  • lagged = (:ion_densities,): the ion densities by name, as :ions for that unknown.

jacobian selects what the stored Jacobian block holds for a flux-limiter scheme (DREAM's ad_jac setting of AdvectionInterpolation). The residual is always the operator's own, so every mode converges to the same solution:

  • :exact: the Jacobian of the residual by colored forward-mode AD, including the limiter's derivative ψ′ (DREAM AD_INTERP_JACOBIAN_FULL, which carries ψ′ only for the limiters DREAM counts as smooth);
  • :frozen_limiter: the limiter weights frozen at the iterate and differentiated as constants (apply!(...; limiter_state), DREAM AD_INTERP_JACOBIAN_LINEAR);
  • a linear scheme name (:upwind, :central, :exponential_fitting, :quick, :upwind_second_order): the Jacobian of the operator with that scheme in p and ξ (with_scheme; :upwind is DREAM AD_INTERP_JACOBIAN_UPWIND), with that scheme's stencil (3-point for the two-point schemes).

For a scheme linear in f the frozen limiter is the exact Jacobian, and another linear scheme's is still a different matrix (upwind's for a central residual, the approximation that rescues some DREAM runs at high momentum resolution with central interpolation; see the docs page Comparison with DREAM). With an approximate block the term declares jacobian_kind(term) === :approximate: the Newton iteration on the stored matrix is a quasi-Newton one that converges linearly, or, with GMRES(operator=:exact), the stored block only preconditions the exact Newton operator (jacobian_correction_product!, one dual-number pass of the operator per product).

Runes2P.KnockOnTerm — Type
KnockOnTerm(primary, operator::KnockOnOperator; target_density, treatment=:explicit_primary)

Knock-on source produced by the kinetic unknown primary on the kinetic row unknown (operator maps the primary grid to the row unknown's grid, per radius, see apply_at_radius!). target_density is the target (total electron) density: the name of a radial unknown, or a vector with one value per radius. Works with different momentum grids at different radii.

treatment:

  • :explicit_primary (default, DREAM's Boltzmann operator MollerBoltzmannOperator): the primary distribution at the previous time level, the target density at the new one. The term is then the target density times a source fixed for the step, evaluated once per residual from the state at the start of the step (prepare_step!), and its Jacobian is the column of the target density at each radius. With a prescribed target density it is the same as :explicit.
  • :explicit: the whole term at the previous time level, target density included.
  • :implicit: the whole term at the new time level, matrix-free, since it couples all cells of a radius. Every Krylov iteration of GMRES applies the operator once, and the direct solver does not take it.

The explicit forms are the cheaper step; the implicit form is the more accurate one per step. The explicit default follows DREAM.

Runes2P.MomentumInterfaceTerm — Type
MomentumInterfaceTerm(lower, upper, lower_term, upper_operator;
                      pitch_mapping=:overlap, scheme=<scheme of the lower operator>)

Particle flux through the momentum face shared by the kinetic unknowns lower and upper: the last p face of the grid of lower (the hot-tail grid) is the first p face of the grid of upper (the runaway grid) at every radius. The pitch grids of the two may differ. The same term goes into both equations. In the equation of lower it removes the flux from the last momentum cells, in the equation of upper it adds the flux to the first momentum cells, and both are computed from the same numbers, so no particle is lost or created at the interface.

lower_term is the Fokker-Planck term of lower (FokkerPlanckTerm or PrescribedFokkerPlanckTerm), or its operator. A prescribed term is passed as the term: a system refuses the bare operator of a prescribed term of the system, whose coefficients belong to the time of its last rebuild. The flux takes the coefficients Aᵖ and Dᵖᵖ of that operator at its last p face, as DREAM's PXiExternalKineticKinetic does, and the two operators add no flux of their own through the face: the lower one has p_upper_boundary = :interface, upper_operator has p_lower_boundary = :interface. When lower_term takes the electric field as an unknown, the advection at the face holds the field of the state, and the term depends on the field as well. The cross term Dᵖᵠ at the face is not included (none in DREAM either, and zero for the collision operator).

pitch_mapping:

  • :overlap (Runes2P): one flux per overlap cell of the two pitch grids (PitchOverlap), from the value of the lower cell and the value of the upper cell the overlap cell lies in,

    Γ_s = flux(Aᵖ_j, Dᵖᵖ_j, f_lower[end, j], f_upper[1, J]; scheme),

    with the two-point flux of scheme between the two cell centres (:upwind, :central or :exponential_fitting, by default the p scheme of the lower operator, and :upwind when that is a four-point scheme). The couplings are those of the scheme between two neighbouring cells, so exponential fitting and upwind keep their sign properties across the interface.

  • :dream: the flux of DREAM, one per pitch cell of the lower grid. The upper value is interpolated linearly in the pitch to the centre of the lower cell, the advection is first-order upwind whatever the scheme of the interior, the diffusion is the difference of the two values over the distance of the centres, and the flux is given to the upper cells by their overlap with the lower cell. Ported as written, including the rule for a lower centre beyond the last upper centre, which takes the second-to-last upper cell (the nearest one at the other end). scheme does not apply. Where the pitch grids differ, the interpolation couples an upper cell to its neighbours in the pitch with negative weights in the diffusive part, so this mapping does not keep the sign of f by construction.

Both conserve particles to round-off. Linear in the state, stored sparse. interface_flux gives the flux through the face as a rate per volume.

Runes2P.MaxwellianSourceTerm — Type
MaxwellianSourceTerm(kinetic, grid, temperature; target_density, rate,
                     bulk_momentum=Inf)

Source with the shape of a non-relativistic Maxwellian exp(-p²/2Θ), Θ = T/mₑc², at temperature [eV] (DREAM's ParticleSourceTerm uses the relativistic one), whose magnitude drives the density of the kinetic unknown kinetic back to target_density (a number, or one value per radius) at the rate rate [1/s]:

S(p, ξ) = rate (n_target - n(f)) f_M(p) / n_M,

with f_M the Maxwellian on grid and n_M its density, so the source adds rate (n_target - n(f)) particles per second and per unit volume and nothing else. kinetic and the row unknown both live on grid. It keeps a thermal bulk from draining into the runaway region, which is what the published Dreicer rates assume; with rate = 1/Δt a step restores the particles the previous step lost.

n(f) counts the cells below bulk_momentum, and n_target is the density of the bulk there. The bound matters: with the whole grid counted, particles moving from the bulk into the runaway tail leave the total unchanged, the source sees no deficit, and the bulk still drains. Set it below the critical momentum and above the thermal momentum.

Runes2P.IonEnergyExchangeTerm — Type
IonEnergyExchangeTerm(plasma; side, ion_energy=:ion_thermal_energy,
                      temperature=:cold_electron_temperature, density=:cold_electron_density,
                      ion_ion=true, ion_coulomb_logarithm=:nrl, masses=nothing, mass_table=:standard,
                      ions=nothing, initial_time=0.0)

The collisional exchange of thermal energy between Maxwellian species (DREAM's src/Equations/Fluid/MaxwellianCollisionalEnergyTransferTerm.cpp, the prefactor at line 49), in W/m³: the power that species a receives from species b,

\[Q_{ab} = \sqrt{3/π}\,\frac{(e^2/ε_0)^2 \sqrt{m_a m_b}}{4π} \ln Λ\, \sqrt{N_a N_b}\, (nZ^2)_a (nZ^2)_b\, \frac{N_a W_b − N_b W_a}{(m_b N_b W_a + m_a N_a W_b)^{3/2}},\]

the two-temperature relaxation of Spitzer (L. Spitzer, Physics of Fully Ionized Gases, 2nd ed., Interscience, 1962), Q_{ab} = (3/2) N_a ν_ε (T_b − T_a) with the energy relaxation rate of the NRL Plasma Formulary (A. S. Richardson, NRL/PU/6770–19-652, Naval Research Laboratory, 2019). For the electrons N = (nZ²) = n_e and W = 3/2 e n_e T_e from the unknowns density and temperature; for an ion species N is its total density, (nZ²) = Σ n_{Z₀} Z₀² (neutrals hold energy and exchange none), and W the component of the unknown ion_energy (J/m³, one component per species of plasma, in its order). The logarithm of electrons and ions is the thermal one, as DREAM's. Among the ions it is

  • ion_coulomb_logarithm = :nrl (default): the mixed ion-ion logarithm of the NRL Plasma Formulary (A. S. Richardson, NRL/PU/6770–19-652, Naval Research Laboratory, 2019, p. 34), λ_ab = 23 − ln[Z_a Z_b (μ_a + μ_b)/(μ_a T_b + μ_b T_a) (Σ_γ n_γ Z_γ²/T_γ)^{1/2}] with the densities in cm⁻³, the temperatures in eV and μ = m/m_p. Formula (c) of the formulary sums over the two colliding ions; its definition of the screening length on the same page sums over every species whose thermal speed exceeds the relative speed of the pair, ū² < kT_γ/m_γ, which holds for the electrons unless the ions are hotter by the mass ratio, so the sum here takes every ion species and the electrons (n_e/T_e). Each ion species takes its own temperature T = W/(3/2 e N) and the electrons theirs, at least the 0.5 eV of DEFAULT_TEMPERATURE_FLOOR, and for Z its mean charge (nZ²)/(nZ), (nZ) = Σ n_{Z₀} Z₀. The formula holds for λ ≫ 1 (its corrections are O(1/λ)); for cold, dense, highly charged ions it falls towards zero and below (D with Ar¹⁶⁺ at 1 eV and 10¹⁹ m⁻³ with electrons at 10 eV: 2.5; Ar¹⁶⁺ with itself at 0.5 eV: −1.3), and it is cut at 0 so that the exchange keeps its sign.

  • ion_coulomb_logarithm = :dream: DREAM's 17.3 − ½ ln(n_free/10²⁰) + 3/2 ln(T_e/1 keV) with the electron temperature and the free-electron density, the same for every pair (src/Equations/CoulombLogarithm.cpp:107-113, with no source named there; BootstrapCurrent.cpp:181 calls the form Wesson's, which takes the ion temperature). With Te in place of Ti it is too small where the ions are hotter than the electrons and too large where they are colder: 10.4 at Te = 10 eV and 10²⁰ m⁻³ against 14.9 of the NRL form for two deuterium species at Ti = 1 keV (the exchange among them 1.4 times too slow after the thermal quench), and against 3.8 for deuterium with Ar⁸⁺ at T_i = 1 eV (the exchange with freshly injected argon 2.7 times too fast).

  • side = :ions, in the row of ion_energy: each component receives Q_{i,e} and, with ion_ion, Σ_j Q_{ij}.

  • side = :electrons, in the row of the temperature (which holds the time derivative of the electron energy): −Σ_i Q_{i,e}.

A system needs both sides on the same unknowns, so the exchange conserves energy; a system with one side alone is refused. The masses are those of ion_mass with mass_table (:standard, the standard atomic weights and the nuclei of the hydrogen isotopes, or :dream, DREAM's rule); masses overrides them per species. The densities of the ions come from the prescribed plasma, a PlasmaState or a function of time, or with ions from that radial unknown of the charge states, whose columns the term then assembles directly; a species of zero density is skipped. ion_ion = true adds the exchange among the ion species, as DREAM does whenever the ion energies are evolved (src/Settings/Equations/T_i.cpp:109). A species of zero total density exchanges nothing and gets no derivative there: the exchange goes as the square root of the density, whose derivative at zero is singular. A Newton iterate with a negative ion energy makes the denominator non-positive, where the exchange is zero with a zero derivative: declare the unknown non-negative, RadialUnknown(:ion_thermal_energy; components, nonnegative = true).

Runes2P.IonRateTerm — Type
IonRateTerm(composition; ions=:ion_densities, temperature=:cold_electron_temperature,
            density=:cold_electron_density, kinetic=nothing, extrapolation=:dream,
            directory=adas_cache_directory())

The ionization and recombination of the charge states of composition (IonComposition) by the cold electrons, in the rows of the ion densities ions (DREAM's IonRateEquation):

\[\frac{dn_j}{dt} = n_e \left(I_{j-1} n_{j-1} - I_j n_j + R_{j+1} n_{j+1} - R_j n_j\right)\]

for each species and charge state j = 0, …, Z, with the ionization coefficients I (ADF11 SCD) and the recombination coefficients R (ACD) of OpenADAS (H. P. Summers, The ADAS User Manual, version 2.6 (2004), http://www.adas.ac.uk, with the data of OpenADAS, https://open.adas.ac.uk) at the electron density n_e and temperature of the radial unknowns density and temperature (adas_coefficient with extrapolation), as DREAM's src/Equations/Fluid/IonRateEquation.cpp. The default extrapolation = :dream takes DREAM's extrapolation below the tables (a choice of this package; see the docs page Choices of this package). The species are named by their element, whose tables are read from directory when the term is built (read_adas).

The sum over the charge states of a species is zero, so the term moves particles between the states and conserves each species. Its Jacobian comes from the colored AD of its residual, with the derivatives of the coefficients through ForwardDiff in place of DREAM's one-sided differences, and without DREAM's floors on the density (1 m⁻³) and the coefficients (1e-40) in the columns of the temperature and the density.

kinetic, a KineticIonizationTerm of the same system, splits the ionization at the threshold of its region: the electrons of f above the threshold ionize through the cross sections of that term, and the cold electrons below it through the fluid coefficient of fluid_ionization_coefficient, the ADAS rate less the part of the Maxwellian above the threshold. No electron is counted twice. The threshold is a multiple of the thermal momentum of at least the factor of MINIMUM_SPLIT_THRESHOLD_FACTORS for the form of its cross sections. Without kinetic the fluid rate is the whole ADAS rate, which next to a kinetic ionization term is DREAM's choice (ions.cpp:280): the Maxwellian tail above the threshold ionizes twice.

Runes2P.KineticIonizationTerm — Type
KineticIonizationTerm(composition, kinetic, grid; ions=:ion_densities, region=nothing,
                      cross_section_form=:chianti)

The ionization of the charge states of composition (IonComposition) by the electrons of the kinetic unknown kinetic on grid, in the rows of the ion densities ions (DREAM's IonKineticIonizationTerm):

\[\frac{dn_j}{dt} = K_{j-1} n_{j-1} - K_j n_j, \qquad K_j = \frac{1}{V'} \sum_{\text{cells}} e(p)\, v σ_j(p)\, 𝒱' Δp Δξ\, f,\]

with the cross sections ionization_cross_section of the form cross_section_form (:chianti, the default, CHIANTI's parameters continued relativistically, with DREAM's form for the elements CHIANTI's table lacks; :dream, DREAM's form and fits; or :bethe, DREAM's form with its relativistic term divided by β², refitted), tabulated at the cell centres of the momentum grid of each radius (DREAM tabulates the grid of radius 0 alone), and the envelope e(p) of the MomentumRegion region: the hot electrons above a threshold that may follow the temperature unknown, or the whole grid without a region. Each species is conserved.

Paired with an IonRateTerm through its kinetic keyword, the electrons below the threshold ionize at the fluid coefficient of fluid_ionization_coefficient, so a Maxwellian f ionizes at the ADAS rate for every threshold. The term has no energy of its own: the friction on bound electrons of the Fokker-Planck term already takes energy from f for the inelastic collisions. With bound_friction = :ionization_split of CollisionSettings and its ionization_threshold at the factor of this term's region, that friction holds the energy I_j + ⟨E_s⟩_j of each ionization the term counts, up to the discretization. Without a region the two agree to the discretization; at a threshold the friction takes a smooth step of its own on the faces (ionization_threshold_width) while this term takes the envelope of its region in the cells, so the two differ by the electrons within that width of the threshold.

The Jacobian is assembled directly: the columns of f (every cell of a radius, the block DREAM's reference case drops), of the charge states, and of the temperature through the threshold of the region.

Runes2P.SecondaryHeatingTerm — Type
SecondaryHeatingTerm(kinetic::KineticIonizationTerm; widths=SECONDARY_SPECTRUM_WIDTHS,
                     temperature=:cold_electron_temperature, cut=:none, spectrum=:beb)

The heating [W/m³] of the cold electrons by the secondary electrons of the kinetic ionization kinetic, for the row of their temperature:

\[Q = e \sum_{s,j} n_{s,j} \frac{1}{V'} \sum_{\text{cells}} e(p)\, v σ_j(p)\, ⟨E_s⟩_j(E)\, 𝒱' Δp Δξ\, f,\]

with the rates of kinetic, the envelope e(p) of its region (1 without one) and the mean energy ⟨E_s⟩_j of the secondaries of the spectrum spectrum and the cut cut below. A kinetically ionized electron joins the cold electrons through the closure of the cold density; without this term it is born at zero energy.

  • cut = :none (the default): the mean over every secondary, up to (E − I)/2. All secondaries join the cold electrons, also those born above the hot threshold, so this is the moment of the spectrum over the secondaries that the cold electrons receive, and the one that the friction with bound_friction = :ionization_split takes from f (CollisionSettings). A KnockOnTerm on the bound electrons also puts the secondaries above its cutoff into f, so next to it their energy is counted twice. Their share of the mean is large: with the spectrum of Opal et al. at 1.3 MeV, the secondaries above 1 keV carry about 59 % of it.
  • cut = :threshold: the mean below the hot threshold E_c, conditional on lying there, for every secondary.

Runes2P.fluid_ionization_coefficient — Method
fluid_ionization_coefficient(adas, fraction, coefficient)

The ionization coefficient of the cold electrons below the threshold of a kinetic ionization: (I − A_>)/(1 − f_>), the ADAS coefficient I of the whole Maxwellian less its part above the threshold, A_>, per electron below it, with the fraction f_> of the Maxwellian above the threshold (threshold_split). With the closure n_cold = n_M (1 − f_>) and the kinetic rate n_M A_> of a Maxwellian f of density n_M, the two add up to n_M I for every threshold.

The coefficient has no floor. A_> comes from the cross sections of the kinetic ionization and I from ADAS. The Maxwellian rate coefficient of DREAM's fits exceeds the ADAS coefficient by up to a factor of about 1.6 for a state of argon, that of CHIANTI's cross sections by up to 2.6 for a state of neon or argon (Ne²⁺ at 3 to 20 eV). Where the threshold lies below the ionization energy of a state, almost all of its Maxwellian ionization comes from above the threshold, so A_> > I and the coefficient is negative. On the grid of the tests (n_e = 5e19 m⁻³, 5 to 50 eV), with DREAM's cross sections, it is negative for the dominant argon states at threshold factors 2 and 3, by 3 to 18 % of I, and for no state with a coronal fraction above 1e-17 at factors 5 and 7. From 1 eV to 10 keV, for D, Ne and Ar, it is negative for no state with a coronal fraction above 1e-3 with DREAM's cross sections at factors 5 to 7. With CHIANTI's it is negative at factor 5 for three such states of a cold plasma: Ar⁺ at 1 eV (−0.19 I, coronal fraction 0.087), Ar²⁺ at 1.6 eV (−0.50 I, 1.2e-3) and Ne²⁺ at 2.5 eV (−2.8 I, 0.025), and for none at factors 6 and 7.
