Runes2P.tanh_p_faces — Method
tanh_p_faces(cell_count, lower_cell_count, p_min, p_separation, p_max;
             transition_cells=cell_count/20)

Faces of a smoothly stretched momentum grid: the biuniform grid of biuniform_p_faces with its spacing jump at p_separation replaced by a tanh transition over about transition_cells cells above it. In the continuous cell index x the spacing is

h(x) = h₁ + (h₂ - h₁) (1 + tanh((x - n₁ - 3w)/w))/2,   n₁ = lower_cell_count, w = transition_cells,

and face k sits at p_min + ∫₀ᵏ h, with h₁ and h₂ chosen so that face n₁ is at p_separation and the last face at p_max. The transition is centred three widths above the separation, so up to p_separation the spacing exceeds h₁ by at most 0.25% of h₂ - h₁ (the bulk keeps its resolution) and it grows over the next few w cells; the upper region needs at least 5w cells. transition_cells = 0 gives the biuniform grid exactly, and the faces approach it linearly in w. The default width, a twentieth of the cells, grows with the cell count, which keeps the grid map smooth under refinement; with a fixed width in cells the max-norm order stalls near 1.5 in the manufactured-solution test. Not available in DREAM, which has uniform, biuniform and custom momentum grids; a DREAM comparison passes these faces as a custom grid.

The biuniform grid converges at second order despite its jump: the local truncation error is O(1) at the jump cell only, and the global error stays second order (test/test_manufactured_solutions.jl). This grid keeps the truncation error second order everywhere when transition_cells grows with the cell count. Where the solution still varies above p_separation, its transition cells, finer than the biuniform grid's upper cells, make it more accurate at equal cell count: 3.5 times on the manufactured solution, 2 times on DREAM's avalanche/generateCylindrical.py with upwind, and no gain on DREAM's runaway/basic.py split at p = 0.1 with the central scheme.

Runes2P.PitchOverlap — Type
PitchOverlap{T}

Overlap of two pitch grids on the same interval, called the left and the right grid (the two sides of a face: the inner and the outer radius, or the lower and the upper momentum grid). The faces of both grids together cut the interval into overlap cells. Overlap cell s lies in exactly one cell of each grid, left_cells[s] and right_cells[s], and the overlap cells of a grid cell j are the contiguous range left_ranges[j] (or right_ranges[j]).

left_fractions[s] is the share of the left cell's measure that lies in overlap cell s, and right_fractions[s] the share of the right cell's. The measure of an interval is its width by default, which is the phase-space volume where 𝒱' does not depend on ξ (cylindrical geometry). measure(a, b) replaces it for a weight that varies with ξ (∫ 𝒱' dξ in toroidal geometry).

A quantity that is one number per overlap cell (a flux through the face, the content moved by a remap) and is given to both sides conserves the total: the left cell j receives Σ_s left_fractions[s] q_s over left_ranges[j] per unit of its measure, the right cell the same with its fractions.

Faces of the two grids closer than 1e-12 of the interval count as one face (the left grid's value is kept), so that faces equal up to round-off, such as cos(π/2) and 0, leave no sliver. The fractions of a cell then sum to 1 within that tolerance, and to round-off otherwise.

Runes2P.remap_to_right! — Method
remap_to_right!(f_right, overlap, f_left)

Conservative remap of cell averages from the left pitch grid of overlap to the right one (remap_to_left! is the reverse), for a vector over the pitch cells or a (p cells, ξ cells) matrix (each momentum cell on its own). The source is taken as constant in each of its cells, and the new cell average is the measure-weighted mean of the source over the cell:

\[f^\mathrm{right}_j = \sum_{s \in \mathtt{right\_ranges}[j]} \mathtt{right\_fractions}[s]\, f^\mathrm{left}_{\mathtt{left\_cells}[s]}.\]

The content Σ_j measure_j f_j is conserved to round-off, a constant stays a constant, the result lies between the smallest and the largest source value (so a non-negative source stays non-negative), and the remap between identical grids is a copy. First order in the cell width: a remap to another grid and back smooths the source. Allocation free. Does not add to the target, it overwrites it.
