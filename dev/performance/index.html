Performance and accuracy

This page has two parts. The first gives the guidelines that hold for every change: how accuracy and cost are weighed, and how a cost is measured. The second holds the details of the hot paths and the linear solvers, with the measurements of the coupled disruption case.

Guidelines

Accuracy first, cost with it

  • A result is trusted because a test holds it: against DREAM with the options that reproduce it, to the tolerance of its solver or closer. Each piece of physics also has a test against a reference independent of DREAM. A faster code path keeps these tests, and the regression values at -O2 bit for bit where it can.
  • A change that moves results at round-off says so, with what was compared and on which case. A sum taken in another order changes the last digits, and a check of single terms does not see an entry that several terms share.
  • A numerical choice is recorded with its reason, and with its cost where it trades accuracy for cost (Choices of this package). A departure from DREAM is judged by its accuracy for its cost. DREAM's behaviour stays an option where it can (Comparison with DREAM).
  • A change that makes a run significantly slower gives its reason in the pull request and is optimized there as far as it can be. Where that is too large for the pull request, an issue takes the rest, with suggestions for the improvement.

Measure, then change

  • The time of a step is in the SolverStatistics of a run: wall_time, and within it jacobian_time, residual_time and linear_solve_time. factorization_time is the part of linear_solve_time spent in the factorizations. They have one entry per attempted step. Their sums over the steps, divided by the sum of newton_iterations, give the cost of a Newton iteration, the unit of this page.
  • A measurement comes after a warm-up of a few steps, so that it leaves out the compilation, and at -O2.
  • Every number names its threads: those of Julia, of BLAS (OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS or BLAS.set_num_threads) and of the solver (threads of MKLPardiso).
  • Two versions are compared in one sitting on an idle machine, alternating: base, head, head, base. Repeated runs of one version differ by a few milliseconds per iteration on the case below, and a gain below that spread is not a gain.
  • Counts do not vary between machines, and they show a change of the cost before a timer does. They are the stored nonzeros (nnz of jacobian_sparsity), the colors of the automatic Jacobian (jacobian_colors) and the Newton iterations per step. The bytes of a second call (@allocated) are such a count too.

Habits of the code

  • A term that knows the entries of its block assembles them itself (jacobian_assembly :direct), above all a row that reads many cells. The colored forward-mode differentiation serves the other terms, the stencil of the kinetic operator among them. A test compares a direct block with the automatic differentiation of its rhs! or with a central-difference Jacobian.
  • The pattern of the stored Jacobian holds the entries that the terms write and no others. Every declared entry costs fill in the factorization, also when it holds a zero.
  • An inner loop has concrete types. A loop over values whose type is known only at run time allocates per entry, so it sits behind a function barrier.
  • A buffer is kept between calls, not allocated per call. A residual or a Jacobian evaluated a second time at one state allocates little, and tests bound it at -O2.
  • A cache is keyed on every input that its values read, such as the plasma values per radius of a PlasmaKey. Its test changes an input alone and counts one rebuild, and none at an unchanged state.
  • The caches and buffers of the terms assume that one task assembles the system, as the package does.

The disruption case

The measurements of this page are of DREAM's disruption case with a runaway grid (The disruption case). It is scenario 1 of DREAM's examples/Disruptions/generate.py, fully kinetic in a cylinder, with the parameters of ASDEXU.py of that example.

QuantityValue
minor radius, wall radius0.5 m, 0.55 m
magnetic field2.5 T, uniform
plasma current800 kA
electron density2.6e19 m⁻³, uniform
central temperature5.8 keV, a Gaussian profile
injected materialdeuterium and argon, each at the density of the background, at 1 eV
heat diffusivity4000 m²/s
hot-tail grid140 momentum cells to p_max = 0.8, 15 pitch cells
runaway grid32 momentum cells from 0.8 to 20, 15 pitch cells (--re-nxi 15 --re-nxi-sep 10 of the case script)
size of the measurements15 radii, 39303 unknowns, 2350635 stored nonzeros

The momenta are in units of $m_e c$. The case has two phases, the ionization of the injected material and the thermal and current quench. The timed window is 10 steps of 6.53e-7 s from step 170 of DREAM's quench. Runes2P starts it from DREAM's state with DREAM's settings (compat = :dream of the builder of the case). A run of 2 steps warms up first, and the timed run starts with a new solver. The tests run the same window at a reduced size, with 2 radii, coarser grids and 835 unknowns (test_disruption_runaway_dream.jl).

Runes2P ran with one Julia thread, one BLAS thread and -O2, DREAM v26.5 with MKL PARDISO; in both codes only PARDISO ran threaded. DREAM's PARDISO allows up to ten refinement steps per solve, and MKLPardiso ran with its default of none. DREAM v26.5 pins PARDISO to one thread, and its rows at eight threads are of a build with that one setting changed. MKL PARDISO is not DREAM's default solver, which is PETSc's LU. The runs were on one idle machine with 8 cores, one at a time, with MKL 2025.2 through Pardiso.jl 1.1.2. The case at this size is not among the reference data: the tests hold the window at the reduced size. The timings and agreements of this page are measurements, and no test repeats them. DREAM's timers are sums over a phase, so its figures are means over its quench, per Newton iteration.

DREAM's Jacobian holds the rebuild of its coefficients, about 31 ms, which Runes2P does inside its residual and its Jacobian. About 5 ms of a Runes2P iteration are outside the four timers. The factorization of MKLPardiso holds its one analysis, in the first timed step; in the later steps the factorization takes about 66 ms per iteration at one thread. The run at eight threads holds one slow residual in one step, without which its iteration is about 70 ms. At the end of the window the states of the two codes agree to 4.1e-9 at this size, the largest difference in the hot-electron density.

With SparseLU the factorization and the solve are 71 % of an iteration, with MKLPardiso 61 % at one thread and 32 % at eight. At one thread the linear solver is therefore the first place to look in a slow run, and the stored pattern decides its cost.

The hot paths

The stored pattern

The kinetic operator declares the stencil of the coefficients it holds (stencil_pattern). An operator without radial transport stores no radial entries, and one without cross diffusion no corners. A coefficient outside the declared stencil is refused where the operator enters a system and when its coefficients are rebuilt. check_stencil_pattern checks in a test that the entries of the matrix lie inside the declared pattern. With the radial entries and corners declared for this case, which fills none of them, UMFPACK would take about seven times as long to factorize.

Colored differentiation and direct rows

The terms with automatic assembly share one coloring, and each color is one directional derivative of their residuals (jacobian_colors). An evaluation in dual numbers carries up to 12 colors. A row that reads many cells of a radius would take a color per cell. Such rows are assembled directly, among them the moments of the density, the current and the energy, and the flux through the interface of two momentum grids. The case has 17 colors, two evaluations; with the interface rows colored it would have 33, three evaluations. A moment keeps the positions and weights of its row for the pattern of the matrix, so a later assembly only adds values.

Plasma columns

The derivatives with respect to the temperature, the density and the charge states are columns of their own (Plasma columns of the Jacobian). The coefficients in dual numbers are built once per state and shared by the term and the terms that read its coefficients. The loops that insert their partials run behind function barriers, with one search of the pattern per radius and column.

Coefficients

The coefficients of a FokkerPlanckTerm are rebuilt only when the plasma values of some radius change (Caches and rebuilds). The slowing down and the energy diffusion come from one sweep over the momentum grid. The factors of the bremsstrahlung and of the bound electrons depend on the momentum and the charge state, not on the plasma, and are tables per momentum grid.

Fluid terms

A term that reads every charge state of a radius assembles its block from one evaluation in dual numbers, seeded at every radius at once (add_local_jacobian!). It keeps its dual buffers between calls, and the tables of the atomic rates have concrete types. The FrictionEnergyTransferTerm keeps its weights under a PlasmaKey. The test of the case bounds the bytes of a second call of the Jacobian, of these blocks and of the residuals of the ion rates.

The linear solvers

  • SparseLU, UMFPACK, is the default and needs no further package. Its symbolic analysis is kept, so each Newton iteration pays the numeric factorization alone.
  • MKLPardiso keeps its ordering and matching in the same way until a solve fails (Choices of this package). Each analysis is counted in the factorization time of its solve. It runs threaded: from one thread to eight its factorization and solve go from 77.6 to 23.1 ms in the table above. DREAM's inversion goes from 88.2 to 45.4 ms.
  • More BLAS threads do not help UMFPACK on this matrix, and ten refinement steps of PARDISO cost more than its factorization. The page Choices of this package gives a factorization and a solve of the stored matrix outside a run, as medians of repeated calls. The figures of the table are per Newton iteration of a run, so the two differ.
  • Both solvers take the field-particle term by a low-rank update of the factorized matrix (low_rank_jacobian), which adds solves and no fill.
  • GMRES with a block preconditioner factorizes blocks in place of the whole matrix (Linear solvers and preconditioners).

Radial transport of the distributions

Radial transport couples the cells of neighbouring radii, and the fill of the factorization grows with it. The same window with Rechester–Rosenbluth diffusion of both distributions at $\delta B/B = 10^{-3}$, in milliseconds per Newton iteration:

DREAM, PARDISO at 1 thread129.94.13096.8, with the solve3231.54.611.00
DREAM, PARDISO at 8 threads138.84.5864.9, with the solve1008.94.611.00
Runes2P, SparseLU59.310.8836.521.1931.94.800.29
Runes2P, MKLPardiso at 1 thread56.211.31029.718.41119.84.800.35
Runes2P, MKLPardiso at 8 threads57.511.9232.48.9314.74.800.31

DREAM's figures are means over the first 238 of the 429 steps of its quench, the part of its run with transport that was timed. In the window itself DREAM takes 4 Newton iterations per step and Runes2P 4.8. The states of the two codes agree to 2.4e-9 at the end of the window, at this size.

  • The factorization and the solve are 92 % of an iteration with SparseLU, and 77 % with MKLPardiso at eight threads.
  • The stored pattern has 2670370 nonzeros and 28 colors, against 2350635 and 17 without transport.
  • At one thread SparseLU is the faster solver with transport, and MKLPardiso without it. With eight threads MKLPardiso is the faster in both.
  • The transport multiplies the factorization of SparseLU by 7.8 and that of MKLPardiso by 14.6 at one thread. DREAM's inversion grows by 35, between means over different spans of its quench.
  • The transport adds 319735 stored nonzeros, and 142800 of them are the corners of the declared cross diffusion, which hold zeros in this case. An operator of radial transport alone built with cross_diffusion = false stores no corners (AdvectionDiffusion). The measurement declared them, and the cost without them is not measured at this size.

What the tests hold

  • The comparison tests with DREAM and the regression values hold the results.
  • test_disruption_runaway_dream.jl bounds the bytes of a second call of jacobian! on the window, of the plasma columns of each term and of the kept blocks. The bounds hold at -O2 and are skipped below it.
  • The blocks of the terms that keep buffers, weights or positions are compared bit for bit with blocks built afresh, at a state and at a changed state.
  • Tests of single systems bound their colors (jacobian_colors), and tests of operators the stored nonzeros of their matrices. No test holds the colors or the stored nonzeros of the disruption case, so a change there is checked by hand.

A change that moves one of these bounds or counts updates it in the same pull request and says so in its description.
