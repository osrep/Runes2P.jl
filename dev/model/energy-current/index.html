Energy and current balance

The electrons exchange energy with the electric field, the ions, the radiation and each other. Their currents set the field through the induction equations. Each term of these balances is a building block of an Equation, and EquationSystem checks that the pieces match.

The energy of the cold electrons

The energy of the cold electrons is an unknown of its own, as in DREAM:

\[W_\mathrm{cold} = \tfrac{3}{2}\, e\, n_\mathrm{cold} T_\mathrm{cold},\]

the algebraic row of thermal_energy_equation with the factor THERMAL_ENERGY_FACTOR. The row of the temperature holds the time derivative of this energy, TimeDerivative(:cold_thermal_energy) (TimeDerivative). Its terms are powers in W/m³, and the temperature follows from the algebraic row. The energy takes its initial value from the temperature and the density (set_thermal_energy!).

The channels of this row:

ChannelTerm
ohmic heating $E j_\mathrm{ohm}$ohmic_heating_term, a ProductTerm
line and recombination radiation, bremsstrahlung, binding energyRadiatedPowerTerm
radial heat transportHeatTransportTerm
exchange with the ionsIonEnergyExchangeTerm with side = :electrons
secondaries of the kinetic ionizationSecondaryHeatingTerm
friction of fluid runawaysRunawayFluidEnergyTransferTerm
collisions of a distribution with the cold electrons, as the energy moment of its electron-electron collisionsfree_electron_energy_transfer_term
friction of the hot-tail distribution on the cold electrons, without the full collision frequenciesFrictionEnergyTransferTerm
electron-electron work on the electrons that cross a momentum interface to a runaway gridInterfaceCollisionWorkTerm

IonEnergyExchangeTerm with side = :ions is the same exchange in the row of the ion energies, so the two sides are antisymmetric. RunawayFluidEnergyTransferTerm is DREAM's heating by runaways without a runaway grid, the power $e c\, E_c\, n_\mathrm{re}$: runaways at the speed of light against the friction of the critical field $E_c$, with the Coulomb logarithm at $p = 20\, m_e c$. With a runaway grid, free_electron_energy_transfer_term takes its place: the energy moment of the electron-electron collisions of that grid.

The row may also hold InterfaceCollisionWorkTerm, which is optional. It belongs there, with coefficient = -1, where a hot-tail grid and a runaway grid share a momentum interface and the row holds free_electron_energy_transfer_term of both grids. Neither moment holds the electron-electron work on the electrons that cross the interface, so without the term the cold electrons miss that work. The test set "the energy budgets of two grids across the interface" of test/test_energy_budget.jl builds no such row: it sums the channels of the two grids by hand. The work is 71 W/m³ and 146 W/m³ at the two radii there, and with it the sum closes to 6.0e-16 of the largest channel.

DREAM's equation of $T_\mathrm{cold}$ (src/Settings/Equations/T_cold.cpp, DREAM v26.5) has every channel of the table but SecondaryHeatingTerm and InterfaceCollisionWorkTerm. For the transfer from a distribution, of the hot-tail grid or of the runaway grid, DREAM takes the form of FrictionEnergyTransferTerm. free_electron_energy_transfer_term is this package's form of the same channel (below). With radiation = :dream and bremsstrahlung_corrections = :dream, RadiatedPowerTerm takes DREAM's radiation. DREAM adds the collisional transfer from a runaway grid in every mode, and from a hot-tail grid in every mode but one:

DREAM's modeTransfer from the hot-tail distributionTransfer from the runaway grid
fully kinetic (collfreq_mode FULL)left outadded
every other modeaddedadded

FrictionEnergyTransferTerm is that transfer from the hot-tail distribution: the moment $\frac{1}{V'} \sum_{ij} m_e c\, v_i p_i\, \nu_s(p_i) f_{ij} \mathcal{V}'_{ij} \Delta p_i \Delta\xi_j$ at the cell centres, with the superthermal frequencies on the cold electrons alone. As a moment of f it takes any scheme of the operator, and it counts the energy of an electron until it leaves the grid, also through $p = 0$ under p_zero_boundary = :outflow. For a runaway grid it is DREAM's form of the transfer, the option next to Runes2P's free_electron_energy_transfer_term (Departures from DREAM). test/test_cold_energy_dream.jl compares the row with ohmic heating, radiation and heat diffusion with DREAM in its fluid mode.

The total energy in the fully kinetic mode

In the fully kinetic mode the hot-tail distribution f covers the bulk. The hot region is the part of the grid above a threshold $p_0$, a MomentumRegion with a ThermalMomentum limit, so that $p_0$ follows $T_\mathrm{cold}$ at each radius. Its electrons hold the energy

\[W_\mathrm{hot} = \frac{1}{V'} \sum_{ij} w_i\, m_e c^2 (\gamma_i - 1)\, f_{ij}\, \mathcal{V}'_{ij} \Delta p_i\, \Delta\xi_j,\]

the EnergyMomentTerm over the hot region, with the envelope $w$ of that region. total_energy_equation adds the unknown

\[W_\mathrm{tot} = W_\mathrm{cold} + W_\mathrm{hot}\]

with its algebraic row. The row of the temperature then holds $\mathrm{d}W_\mathrm{tot}/\mathrm{d}t$, TimeDerivative(:total_electron_energy), and the temperature follows from $W_\mathrm{cold} = W_\mathrm{tot} - W_\mathrm{hot}$.

The row lists only the channels by which energy enters or leaves the electrons of the hot-tail grid:

  • The work of the field is $E j_\mathrm{ohm} + E j_\mathrm{hot}$ (two ProductTerms), where the ohmic current already leaves out the hot current, so each electron is counted once.
  • The radiation, the heat transport and the exchange with the ions are as in the row of the cold energy, except for the Rechester-Rosenbluth heat transport next to radial transport of f (below).
  • The heating by the secondaries of a kinetic ionization over the hot region (SecondaryHeatingTerm) moves their energy from the sinks to the cold electrons.
  • The sinks of the collisions of f are KineticEnergyLossTerm with part = :sinks over the hot region.
  • The outflow at $p_\mathrm{max}$ is MomentumBoundaryFluxTerm with weight = :energy and coefficient = -1, one for each Fokker-Planck term of f with collisions or with the field as an unknown.
  • The channel of the electrons that cross to a runaway grid is InterfaceEnergyFluxTerm, with the work of the electron-electron collisions on them, InterfaceCollisionWorkTerm (below).
  • The energy of the knock-ons that the fluid runaways above the grid create (RosenbluthPutvinskiSourceTerm) is the KineticEnergyChangeTerm of that source, over the whole grid.
  • The energy that radial transport of f moves at each radius is the KineticEnergyChangeTerm of the FokkerPlanckTerm of the transport, over the whole grid (below).

The collisional transfer from the hot electrons to the cold ones does not appear in the row, and the system refuses it there (below). Neither do the electrons that cross the threshold in momentum at one radius, nor the threshold that moves with the temperature. The time derivative acts on the energy of the hot region itself, so these exchanges inside $W_\mathrm{tot}$ at one radius are exact for any time stepper. Radial transport moves electrons between radii, so it is not among these exchanges, and the row holds it as a term of its own (below).

What the system enforces

Every change of $W_\mathrm{hot}$ that the row does not list is charged to the cold electrons. EquationSystem therefore checks the row against the equation of f. The list below gives what it checks, by term type and by boundary condition. A term of a type that no item names passes without a check, and the change of $W_\mathrm{hot}$ that it makes goes to the cold electrons. The docstring of total_energy_equation gives the conditions.

  • Collisions with sinks (synchrotron loss, bremsstrahlung stopping power, partial or no screening) remove energy from f, so the row needs the sinks term.
  • The electrons that leave a Fokker-Planck term at $p_\mathrm{max}$ take their energy with them, and the row needs the energy flux of that boundary. This holds for a term with collisions, and for a term without collisions that carries the field as an unknown.
  • The electrons that cross the interface of a Fokker-Planck term with collisions to a runaway grid take their energy with them, and the row needs the energy flux of that interface. It also needs one InterfaceCollisionWorkTerm of the same interface, with a negative coefficient (below). The row refuses the term of an interface whose lower distribution, that of the grid below the face, is not in $W_\mathrm{hot}$. A second InterfaceCollisionWorkTerm of one interface, which would count the work twice, is refused in every row, the row of the cold thermal energy alone included.
  • Radial transport of f changes the energy of f at each radius. A FokkerPlanckTerm of f whose operator holds nonzero radial coefficients requires one KineticEnergyChangeTerm of that very term in the row, without a region. A Fokker-Planck term whose operator declares radial transport but holds no radial coefficients yet, as one built for a RechesterRosenbluthUpdate, does not yet require it. A run checks the radial coefficients again before its first step, so the update passes only when the row holds the term from the build of the system.
  • The system checks that the coefficient of that energy term is positive, and a coefficient of 1 closes the budget. A second energy term of one Fokker-Planck term is refused, and so is one whose Fokker-Planck term is not in the equation of a distribution in $W_\mathrm{hot}$. Next to it, a HeatTransportTerm with the Rechester-Rosenbluth diffusivity is refused, since it would move the heat of the bulk a second time.
  • A FokkerPlanckTerm of f without collisions, with no plasma and no coefficient source, is refused when its operator holds coefficients in momentum that change the energy of f. These are the advection and diffusion in $p$, the cross diffusion of the flux in $p$, and a flux through $p_\mathrm{min}$ or $p = 0$. No term of the row carries that energy. A run checks the coefficients again before its first step. Pitch coefficients move no energy and stay allowed in such a term.
  • A term without collisions may carry the field as an unknown (electric_field of FokkerPlanckTerm). Its advection is then the field times the coefficients of a unit field, 1 V/m (set_unit_field_coefficients!), and these coefficients are allowed. Their work is the term $E j_\mathrm{hot}$, which the row must hold, and the system does not check that the row holds it. Such a term is refused in a distribution with an interface to a runaway grid, whatever its own p_upper_boundary, since the electrons that its field moves would not cross the interface. Put the field on the Fokker-Planck term with collisions at the interface instead.
  • A nonzero prescribed flux through $p_\mathrm{min}$ (p_lower_boundary = :prescribed) of a Fokker-Planck term with collisions is refused, since no term of the row carries the energy of the electrons that it brings. A zero p_flux_at_lower_boundary, or p_lower_boundary = :zero_flux, passes.
  • A KnockOnTerm on the grid is refused, since no term carries the energy of the secondaries.
  • A RosenbluthPutvinskiSourceTerm creates knock-ons whose energy no other term carries. The row needs one KineticEnergyChangeTerm of that very source, without a region (below). The system checks that the coefficient is positive, and a coefficient of 1 closes the budget. The row refuses a KineticEnergyChangeTerm whose source is not in the equation of a distribution in $W_\mathrm{hot}$, since the energy that it counts is outside $W_\mathrm{tot}$.
  • A LossRateTerm and a MaxwellianSourceTerm are refused, since no term carries the energy of the electrons that they remove or add.
  • A LinearTerm with a nonzero coefficient in the equation of f is refused, since it moves electrons into f from a kinetic unknown or out of it.
  • The row refuses the transfer of f to the cold electrons: KineticEnergyLossTerm with part = :free_electrons, free_electron_energy_transfer_term and FrictionEnergyTransferTerm of f. That transfer stays inside $W_\mathrm{tot}$, and $W_\mathrm{cold} = W_\mathrm{tot} - W_\mathrm{hot}$ already receives it, so the term would count it twice. The same terms of a runaway grid outside $W_\mathrm{tot}$ stay allowed.
  • The row refuses the sinks term and the energy flux through $p_\mathrm{max}$ of a distribution outside $W_\mathrm{tot}$, such as a runaway grid. That distribution loses the energy itself, so the cold electrons would pay it as well.
  • The row refuses a SecondaryHeatingTerm whose kinetic ionization of f takes another region than the energy moment of f, wider or narrower. Such a heating gives the cold electrons the energy of secondaries whose ionizations the sinks term does not charge, or leaves out secondaries that it does charge. Without bound_friction = :ionization_split, the remedy is the region of the energy moment on the KineticIonizationTerm. Under the split, which requires the heating where the row holds the sinks term, the ionization takes a ThermalMomentum threshold, a factor times the thermal momentum. The remedy there gives the energy moment a ThermalMomentum region, the ionization the same region, and ionization_threshold of CollisionSettings its factor.

Each refused channel would take its energy from the cold electrons, or give it to them, at the radius where the electrons are moved, created or removed.

The check has two known gaps. The change of $W_\mathrm{hot}$ that a FieldParticleTerm in the equation of f makes is not checked and lands in $W_\mathrm{cold}$. Such a term should not be combined with a row of the total energy until a term of the row carries its energy. The second gap is a $W_\mathrm{hot}$ made of the runaway grid alone, fed through p_lower_boundary = :interface from a grid outside $W_\mathrm{tot}$, which the system accepts unchecked.

A ParticleSourceTerm is accepted. The cold density is the closure $n_\mathrm{cold} = n_\mathrm{free} - n_\mathrm{hot} - n_\mathrm{re}$. Here $n_\mathrm{hot}$ is the density of the hot region (DensityMomentTerm over it), and $n_\mathrm{re}$ the runaway density where the system has one. With the constraint $0 = n(f) - n_\mathrm{cold} - n_\mathrm{hot}$ for its magnitude, which keeps $n(f)$ at $n_\mathrm{free} - n_\mathrm{re}$, the source moves electrons between the cold population and f. The cold electrons therefore rightly pay the energy that its electrons bring into $W_\mathrm{hot}$. A MaxwellianSourceTerm adds or removes electrons from outside the system, so no population of the system should pay or gain their energy.

The row creates the energy of the knock-ons of the Rosenbluth-Putvinski source in the system, a choice of this package (Choices of this package). The primaries of the source are the fluid runaways above the grid, which have no energy unknown, so no population pays that energy. The alternative, which the package rejects, charges it to the cold electrons, as a row without KineticEnergyChangeTerm would. The term takes no region, since every knock-on is in $W_\mathrm{tot}$. A secondary below the threshold of the hot region heats the cold electrons, and one above it joins $W_\mathrm{hot}$.

The term overlaps with the heating by fluid runaways, RunawayFluidEnergyTransferTerm. That heating gives the cold electrons $e c\, E_c\, n_\mathrm{re}$, the whole collisional stopping power of the runaways on the free electrons. The knock-ons between the cutoff $p_\mathrm{cut}$ of the source and $p_\mathrm{max}$ take their energy from that loss, so with both terms in the row $W_\mathrm{tot}$ gains their energy twice. DREAM has both terms too, in separate reservoirs: its equation of $T_\mathrm{cold}$ holds $e c\, E_c\, n_\mathrm{re}$ and no term of the source.

Where all free electrons are cold and $n_\mathrm{tot}$ equals their density, the ratio of the power $P$ of the knock-on term to the heating is, in the limit of fine momentum cells,

\[\frac{P}{e c\, E_c\, n_\mathrm{re}} = \frac{\ln[(\gamma_\mathrm{max} - 1)/(\gamma_\mathrm{cut} - 1)]}{2 \ln\Lambda_{ee}(p = 20)},\]

with $\gamma_\mathrm{cut}$ and $\gamma_\mathrm{max}$ the Lorentz factors at $p_\mathrm{cut}$ and $p_\mathrm{max}$, and $\ln\Lambda_{ee}(p = 20)$ the Coulomb logarithm of the heating. At $p_\mathrm{cut} = 0.01$, $p_\mathrm{max} = 0.8$, 10 eV and $10^{20}\, \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, with the :energy_dependent logarithm, the ratio is 0.251.

The term does not remove the thermal energy of the target electron. Take the closure of the cold density and the constraint of a ParticleSourceTerm (above). Each knock-on above the threshold of the hot region then takes one electron out of the cold population, while $W_\mathrm{cold}$ keeps its thermal energy $\tfrac{3}{2} T$. The cold electrons that remain therefore gain $\tfrac{3}{2} T$ per such knock-on. That is 0.62 % of the term at 10 eV, $p_\mathrm{cut} = 0.04$ and $p_\mathrm{max} = 0.8$, where every knock-on lies above the threshold. A bound target, which $n_\mathrm{tot}$ counts, does not pay its ionization energy either, and DREAM behaves the same in both respects.

Radial transport of the kinetic electrons

Radial transport of f, such as the diffusion along stochastic field lines of add_rechester_rosenbluth_diffusion!, is a FokkerPlanckTerm of its own. Under the total energy its operator holds radial and pitch coefficients alone: no term of the row carries the energy that coefficients in momentum would move, and the system refuses them (above). Its KineticEnergyChangeTerm is the power density $P_k$ [W/m³] that the radial coefficients of the operator give the electrons of f at radius $k$, over the whole grid:

\[P_k = -\frac{1}{V'_k \Delta r_k} \sum_{ij} m_e c^2 (\gamma_i - 1)\, \Delta p_i\, \Delta\xi_j \left(\Phi_{ij,k+1} - \Phi_{ij,k}\right).\]

$P_k$ is positive where the electrons at radius $k$ gain energy. $V'_k$ is the spatial Jacobian of the radius ($V' = r$ in a cylinder) and $\Delta r_k$ its width. $\Phi_{ij,k} = \mathcal{V}'_{ij,k} \Gamma^r_{ij,k}$ is the radial flux of the cell $(i, j)$ through face $k$, the inner face of radius $k$, positive outward. $\mathcal{V}'_{ij,k}$ is the phase-space Jacobian on that face (phase_space_jacobian_r_faces). $\Gamma^r = A^r f - D^{rr} \partial_r f$ is the radial flux density of AdvectionDiffusion, and face $N + 1$ is the edge of the plasma, with $N$ the number of radii.

The fluxes are those of the discretised equation of f. $P_k$ is therefore the change that the transport makes to the energy of f at radius $k$, to round-off. The term takes the whole grid, a choice of this package (Choices of this package). The transport only moves energy between the radii and out through the edge. The volume integral $\sum_k V'_k \Delta r_k P_k$ is minus the energy flux out through the edge, the energy that the plasma loses there.

That integral is zero for an operator with a closed edge, r_boundary = :zero_flux of AdvectionDiffusion, whatever the threshold of the hot region at each radius. The row holds $P_k$. $W_\mathrm{hot}$ changes by the same moment over the hot region alone, with the envelope $w_{i,k}$ of that region, from 0 below the threshold to 1 above it. Since $W_\mathrm{cold} = W_\mathrm{tot} - W_\mathrm{hot}$, $W_\mathrm{cold}$ changes by the moment with the weight $1 - w_{i,k}$: the energy that the transport moves with the electrons below the threshold.

An electron that the transport carries from radius $k$, where it lies above the threshold, to radius $k + 1$, where it lies below it, shows how this works:

  1. At radius $k$ the electron leaves $W_\mathrm{hot}$, and $W_\mathrm{tot}$ loses the same energy through $P_k$, so $W_\mathrm{cold}$ there receives nothing from it.
  2. At radius $k + 1$ it lies below the threshold, so $W_\mathrm{hot}$ does not change, while $P_{k+1}$ adds its energy to $W_\mathrm{tot}$.
  3. Its energy therefore enters $W_\mathrm{cold}$ at radius $k + 1$.

The cold density does not follow the electrons below the threshold, since it is the closure of the cold density (above), which reads $n_\mathrm{hot}$ and not $n(f)$. $T_\mathrm{cold}$ therefore rises where the transport brings their energy and falls where it takes it. Where the transport changes $n_\mathrm{hot}$, $n_\mathrm{cold}$ changes by the opposite amount, and that part of the change brings no energy to $W_\mathrm{cold}$, as for a knock-on (above).

A ParticleSourceTerm adds electrons to f with its shape, by default a Maxwellian at $T_\mathrm{cold}$, at the rate of its magnitude, and the row has no term for their energy. Its part above the threshold changes $W_\mathrm{hot}$ and is charged to the cold electrons, and its part below the threshold changes no term of the row. With the constraint above for its magnitude, the source refills f where the transport takes electrons out of it and drains it where the transport brings them.

HeatTransportTerm is a separate model of the heat transport of the cold electrons, the diffusion of $T_\mathrm{cold}$. Its Rechester-Rosenbluth diffusivity is the energy moment of the kinetic diffusion of add_rechester_rosenbluth_diffusion! on a Maxwellian of uniform density. Next to the energy term of the transport of f, that diffusivity would move the heat of the bulk a second time, so the row of the total energy refuses the pair, whatever its level. On a Maxwellian bulk of uniform density at a temperature profile on 8 radii, the two agree to 1 % at the six inner radii and to 13 % at the seventh, near the edge (test/test_heat_transport.jl). A prescribed diffusivity of HeatTransportTerm stays allowed, as a heat transport of the user's own on top of the kinetic transport.

Where the density has a gradient, the energy term of the transport of f carries heat even at a uniform temperature, unlike the heat transport of the temperature. The diffusion of f has no ambipolar constraint, so it moves electrons down the gradient of the density, with their energy. The particle source refills f where the electrons leave and drains it where they arrive, and that refill carries no energy in the row (above).

At a uniform 100 eV under the Rechester-Rosenbluth diffusion, the term carries an energy of 2.0 times the temperature per transported electron (test/test_heat_transport.jl). That measured value equals the analytic ratio of the energy flux to the particle flux, $2T$, for a flux proportional to $v$ over a Maxwellian. The heat transport of the temperature carries none there. The test takes $n = 10^{19} (3 - 2 r^2/a^2)\, \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, with $a$ the minor radius, on 8 radii and at $\delta B/B = 2 \times 10^{-3}$. There the term is −7.329e5 W/m³ at each of the seven inner radii, where the density is high, and +2.394e6 W/m³ at the outermost radius.

The Rechester-Rosenbluth diffusion grows as $|v_\parallel| \propto \sqrt{T}$, so its particle flux follows the gradient of $n \sqrt{T}$ and its energy flux that of $n T^{3/2}$. The term therefore vanishes at a uniform $n T^{3/2}$, and the cold electrons tend towards that state, not towards a uniform $T$. That equilibrium follows from the ratio of the fluxes, not from a computed time evolution. A radial diffusion independent of $p$ carries 1.5 times the temperature per transported electron in the same test, and tends towards a uniform $n T$.

A row of the total energy has no remedy for this in the package. A row of the cold thermal energy with HeatTransportTerm and the Rechester-Rosenbluth diffusivity gives a uniform $T$.

test/test_energy_transfer.jl compares the term with the moment of the operator applied to f, and test/test_energy_budget.jl closes the budget of $W_\mathrm{tot}$ at each radius and in the volume with it.

Why the hot-tail grid only

The runaway grid stays outside $W_\mathrm{tot}$. A relative error $\varepsilon$ of $W_\mathrm{tot}$ becomes a relative error of the cold energy of

\[\varepsilon\, \frac{W_\mathrm{tot}}{W_\mathrm{cold}},\]

so the temperature is lost to cancellation when $W_\mathrm{tot}$ is much larger than $W_\mathrm{cold}$. Keeping the runaways out keeps this ratio small. The runaway grid enters the row through its collisions with the cold electrons, free_electron_energy_transfer_term or FrictionEnergyTransferTerm. It also enters through InterfaceEnergyFluxTerm for the electrons that it receives, and InterfaceCollisionWorkTerm for the work of the collisions on those electrons.

The field work on the hot region

The row charges $E j_\mathrm{hot}$ for the work of the field on the hot region. The energy moment of the discrete field term over that region differs from it. The difference includes the energy $E_k(p_0)\, \Gamma$, with $E_k(p_0) = m_e c^2 (\gamma(p_0) - 1)$ and $\Gamma$ the flux of electrons that the field moves across the threshold $p_0$, which the total energy takes from the cold electrons. test/test_energy_budget.jl compares the two in a driven fully kinetic plasma, next to the transfer from a slowing-down tail and the checks of the row.

Energy transfer between the distributions

KineticEnergyLossTerm is the energy moment of a part of the collisions in a FokkerPlanckTerm:

\[L = -\frac{1}{V'} \sum_{ij} w_i\, m_e c^2 (\gamma_i - 1)\, \left[C_a f - C_b f\right]_{ij} \mathcal{V}'_{ij} \Delta p_i \Delta\xi_j,\]

with the envelope $w$ of the region.

  • part = :free_electrons: $C_a$ the electron-electron collisions (electron_electron_operator), $C_b = 0$. This is the energy that f gives to the free cold electrons, free_electron_energy_transfer_term.
  • part = :sinks: $C_a$ all collisions without the field, $C_b$ their electron-electron part. This is the energy that leaves the electrons by the friction on bound electrons, bremsstrahlung and synchrotron radiation.

Neither part takes a flux through $p_\mathrm{max}$, whatever the upper boundary of the Fokker-Planck term. The energy of the electrons that cross there, that of the last momentum cell, belongs to the boundary term alone, so it is not counted twice, and the cold electrons do not exchange it. That term is MomentumBoundaryFluxTerm at an outer boundary and InterfaceEnergyFluxTerm at the interface to a runaway grid.

With the sinks charged to $W_\mathrm{tot}$, the cold electrons receive exactly the free-electron part of what the hot electrons lose, where the parts sum to the momentum flux. The parts are the field part, the flux of the electric field alone, the free-electron part and the sinks. They sum to the momentum flux of the Fokker-Planck term under every momentum scheme but exponential fitting, and under exponential fitting without a field (below). Under exponential fitting with a field they do not, the rest goes to the cold electrons, and the system does not refuse it.

With a field the cold electrons receive in addition the gap between $E j_\mathrm{hot}$ and the work of the discrete field term (The field work on the hot region). In a test of a field term without collisions at 50 V/m, with a constant 1e16 added to every cell of f up to $p_\mathrm{max}$, $|dW_\mathrm{cold}/dt|$ divided by the energy flux through $p_\mathrm{max}$ is 2.7e-4 (test/test_energy_budget.jl).

The momentum face values of the parts take the upwind side of the whole advection of the Fokker-Planck term, the sum of its collisions and its electric field. This holds under every momentum scheme whose face value has a side: :upwind, :quick, :upwind_second_order and the flux limiters. The four-point schemes among them have a side on a grid of at least 3 momentum cells, and take the central face value on fewer. The field part is the flux of the electric field alone, the flux of the whole less those of the free-electron part and the sinks, and it takes the same side. That face value depends on $f$ and on the side alone, so the field part, the free-electron part and the sinks sum to the momentum flux of the term face by face.

With each operator of a part on the side of its own advection, the parts would miss that flux at every face where the advection of the operator and the whole advection have opposite signs. This happens where the field beats the friction. It also happens at the face $p = 0$ under a four-point scheme with p_zero_boundary = :outflow, where the friction vanishes and the field alone sets the side. In a test of a 100 eV plasma at 2 V/m (test/test_energy_transfer.jl), their sum then differs from the flux of the whole by up to 0.18 of the largest face flux of the whole under the flux limiters. Under the linear schemes with a side it differs by up to 0.44 of that flux.

The central scheme and exponential fitting in $p$ have no side. With linear schemes in $\xi$ and $r$ the parts then take the moment of their operators applied to $f$. The central face value is the same on either side, so the parts sum to the flux under it as well.

With a field, exponential fitting in $p$ is the exception to that sum: its face value follows the coefficients of each operator, so the parts do not sum to the flux there. This holds with or without a limiter in $\xi$ or $r$, and for the faces inside a grid. Without a field the field part is zero, the first operator of the sinks holds the same coefficients as the Fokker-Planck term, and the free-electron part and the sinks sum to its flux. At the interface to a runaway grid every part takes the one face value of the whole flux, so the parts sum to the flux there under every scheme (The energy step at the interface).

Under a momentum scheme with a side, and under a flux limiter in any direction, the term takes the moment in the form of the momentum faces, since the pitch fluxes sum to zero over a momentum row:

\[L = -\frac{1}{V'} \sum_j \Delta\xi_j \sum_k \left(\varepsilon_k - \varepsilon_{k-1}\right) F_{kj},\]

with $\varepsilon_k$ the energy $w_k m_e c^2 (\gamma_k - 1)$ of momentum cell $k$, $\varepsilon_0 = 0$, and $F_{kj}$ the momentum flux of $C_a - C_b$ through the lower face of cell $(k, j)$, multiplied by $\mathcal{V}'$. The term reads the sign of the whole advection, not of the field alone, and has no column of the field unknown. Where a face changes its side, the whole advection passes through zero, so the momentum flux of the Fokker-Planck term, the sum of the parts, stays continuous. The advection of each part does not vanish there, so each part jumps as its face value moves from one cell to the other.

With a row that feeds back on the side, Newton's method may not converge on an implicit step that straddles a change of side. An example is the temperature of the cold electrons heated by a runaway grid, since the friction that sets the side follows that temperature. Newton's iteration then alternates between the two sides, the step control halves the step, and the run continues with the result of the smaller steps. test/test_runaway_heating_dream.jl shows this under :upwind and under the flux limiter :tcdf.

MomentumBoundaryFluxTerm with weight = :energy and InterfaceEnergyFluxTerm weight the flux at the top of the grid with the kinetic energy of the last momentum cell. That is the energy that the discrete energy moment of the grid loses with the electrons. The electrons that cross to the runaway grid take that energy with them and do not heat the cold ones with it. test/test_energy_transfer.jl and test/test_energy_moment.jl test these terms and the energy moment, and test/test_friction_energy_transfer.jl tests FrictionEnergyTransferTerm.

The side on a coarse momentum grid

This subsection shows how the shared side changes the free-electron part on a coarse momentum grid, and how both choices of the side converge.

The shared side is the upwind side of the whole advection, which all parts take. Where the field beats the friction at a face, the face value of every part there is that of the cell below the face. The friction of the free electrons then carries electrons of that cell down through the face, and the term counts that flux with the step of the energy between the two cells. On its own side the friction would take the face value of the cell above the face, and would carry the electrons of a cell down through the face below it instead. On a coarse momentum grid the two give different energies to the cold electrons.

The table holds the free-electron part divided by the friction moment at the cell centres. That moment is FrictionEnergyTransferTerm with the collision settings of the grid, on the same grid and state, and it is not an exact value. The state is the start of the test case runaway_heating_example at compat = :runes2p, at 100 eV and 1.5 V/m. In it $f = 1$ in the momentum cells below $p = 0.5\, m_e c$ of the last pitch cell and zero elsewhere, and only the width of the momentum cells varies. The column "Each part on its own side" is the moment of the operator applied to $f$, each face on the side of the advection of its own operator, which the term does not take.

The energy step at the interface

An electron that crosses the interface leaves the hot-tail grid with the kinetic energy $E_{\mathrm{low},N}$ of its last momentum cell. It enters the runaway grid with the energy $E_{\mathrm{up},1}$ of the first momentum cell there. The step $E_{\mathrm{up},1} - E_{\mathrm{low},N}$ is about $m_e c^2 \beta\, \delta p$, with $\beta = v/c$ at the face and $\delta p$ the distance of the two cell centres in units of $m_e c$. It therefore vanishes as the cells at the face are refined.

Neither the energy moments of the two operators nor the sinks term take a flux through the face, so no operator pays the step. Each part of the flux through the face pays it for its electrons. A payer loses the step when the flux of its part goes up through the face, and gains it when that flux goes down.

The ions scatter the electrons in pitch only, so they move none across the face. The step of the sinks changes the energy of the runaway grid alone, which is outside $W_\mathrm{tot}$, so the row takes no term for it.

The row credits $W_\mathrm{tot}$ with the field work $E j_\mathrm{hot}$, and the hot current $j_\mathrm{hot}$ covers the cells of f up to the face. $W_\mathrm{hot}$ itself gains the work of the discrete field term of f only up to the centre of its last cell. At the face it loses $\Gamma E_{\mathrm{low},N}$, with $\Gamma$ the flux through the face. So $W_\mathrm{tot}$ holds about $\Gamma_E (E_\mathrm{face} - E_{\mathrm{low},N})$ that $W_\mathrm{hot}$ never receives, with $\Gamma_E$ the field part of the flux and $E_\mathrm{face}$ the kinetic energy at the face. Since $W_\mathrm{cold} = W_\mathrm{tot} - W_\mathrm{hot}$, the cold electrons receive it as heat.

The runaway grid receives the whole step with the electron, so that part of the step is counted twice. The part is positive where the field drives electrons up through the face, and negative where it drives them down. It is roughly the fraction $\Delta p_\mathrm{low}/(\Delta p_\mathrm{low} + \Delta p_\mathrm{up})$ of the step of the field, with $\Delta p_\mathrm{low}$ and $\Delta p_\mathrm{up}$ the widths of the two cells at the face, and it falls with the width of these cells. The row has no term for it, as for the gap at the threshold of the hot region (The field work on the hot region).

Each part takes the face value of the whole flux: the upwind side, the linear interpolation or the weight of exponential fitting that the coefficients of all processes set. The parts therefore add up to the flux through the face, as the docstring of InterfaceCollisionWorkTerm shows.

The term charges the work of the electron-electron collisions to the cold electrons, a choice of this package (Choices of this package). The cold electrons are the targets of these collisions, and inside each grid they gain the energy that the collisions take from the electrons. Where the friction pulls electrons down through the face, the cold electrons gain the step of each of them, and where the energy diffusion pushes electrons up, they pay it.

test/test_energy_budget.jl closes the energy of the two grids across the face under a field, with the whole step of the field at the face written out as a channel of its own and without the row of the total energy. Without a field, it conserves the total energy of a two-grid system through the row of the total energy with the term. Both tests also show what they miss without the term. With a field, the row of the total energy counts the part of the step of the field up to the face twice, as described above.

The energy of the kinetic ionization

A kinetic ionization (KineticIonizationTerm) has no energy of its own: the friction on bound electrons of the Fokker-Planck term takes the energy of the ionization from f. With bound_friction = :ionization_split of CollisionSettings, that friction charges f the energy $I_k + \langle E_s \rangle_k$ of each ionization of the charge state $k$, with $I_k$ its ionization potential and $\langle E_s \rangle_k$ the mean energy of its secondary. The potential is stored in the ions and released at recombination (RadiatedPowerTerm with radiation = :recombination). The secondaries join the cold electrons through the closure of the cold density, and SecondaryHeatingTerm with cut = :none gives them their energy. ionization_energy_flows separates the two parts.

EquationSystem requires the heating by secondaries where the sinks of f are booked with this friction, with the same spectrum and cut = :none or cut = :knockon. With DREAM's bound_friction = :bethe, the friction does not hold the energy of each ionization, so the requirement does not apply (KineticEnergyLossTerm).

Next to a KnockOnTerm on the bound electrons, SecondaryHeatingTerm with cut = :none counts the energy of the secondaries above the knock-on cutoff twice, since the knock-on term also puts them into a distribution. With cut = :knockon and the knock-on term as knockon_term, the heating gives the cold electrons only the energy of the secondaries below the knock-on cutoff at each radius (secondary_energy_below, beb_secondary_energy_below). The heating reads the cutoff at each evaluation, so it follows KnockOnCutoffUpdate.

The system refuses cut = :none and cut = :threshold next to a knock-on term of the same primary whose target density is the unknown :total_electron_density, which counts the bound electrons. With cut = :knockon, knockon_term must then be such a term. The system refuses cut = :knockon with a knock-on term on :cold_electron_density or :runaway_density, which count free electrons only, since no term then puts the secondaries above the cutoff into a distribution. A target density given as values or under another name is not checked, since the system cannot tell which electrons it counts.

Under the total energy the system refuses the knock-on term.

Currents and conductivity

The total current density is the sum

\[j_\mathrm{tot} = j_\mathrm{ohm} + j_\mathrm{hot} + j_\mathrm{re},\]

each an algebraic unknown. The hot current is the CurrentMomentTerm of f over the hot region. The runaway current is the current moment of the runaway grid, or $e c\, n_\mathrm{re}$ of a fluid runaway density.

The ohmic current takes a ConductivityTerm with a ConductivityModel:

  • fluid electrons: $j_\mathrm{ohm} = \sigma E$, with the conductivity of Braams and Karney (braams_karney_conductivity), which is DREAM's conductivity of Sauter and Redl in a cylinder.
  • fully kinetic: $j_\mathrm{ohm} = j(f) - j_\mathrm{hot} + E (\sigma - \sigma_\mathrm{kin})$, DREAM's corrected conductivity.

The conductivity of Braams and Karney: B. J. Braams, C. F. F. Karney, Phys. Fluids B 1, 1355 (1989), doi:10.1063/1.858966. The conductivity of Sauter: O. Sauter, C. Angioni, Y. R. Lin-Liu, Phys. Plasmas 6, 2834 (1999), doi:10.1063/1.873240. The formulae of Redl: A. Redl et al., Phys. Plasmas 28, 022502 (2021), doi:10.1063/5.0012664.

The test-particle operator does not conserve the momentum of the electron-electron collisions, so its conductivity is below $\sigma$. The correction adds the difference. Two models of $\sigma_\mathrm{kin}$:

  • kinetic = :dream_fit: DREAM's fit of the conductivity of its kinetic operator (test_particle_conductivity_fraction).
  • kinetic = :discrete_operator: the conductivity of the discrete operator of the run (kinetic_conductivity), by its linear response to a small field.

With :discrete_operator, the steady state of a small field has the reference conductivity whatever the grid and the collision settings are. The correction is a statement on the steady state: for some collision times after a change of the field, the current stays below $\sigma E$. With its heat-flux modes, the momentum-conserving FieldParticleTerm brings the conductivity of the distribution close to that of Spitzer and Härm, for a nonrelativistic bulk. The conductivity of Spitzer and Härm: L. Spitzer, R. Härm, Phys. Rev. 89, 977 (1953), doi:10.1103/PhysRev.89.977. The system refuses it next to a correction. The system also refuses a :discrete_operator model whose pitch rule differs from that of the CurrentMomentTerm in the same equation.

The ohmic heating ohmic_heating_term reads the same ohmic current, so the heating and the current agree by construction. test/test_conductivity.jl tests the conductivity against Spitzer and Härm and the Lorentz gas, the corrected conductivity in steady state and transient, and the resistive decay of a kinetic run against a fluid one.

Induction

induction_unknowns and induction_equations give the induction equations in cylindrical geometry, with DREAM's pairing of rows and unknowns. The poloidal flux $\psi$ is divided by the major radius, as in DREAM.

UnknownEquation
electric field $E$$\mathrm{d}\psi/\mathrm{d}t = 2\pi E$ (Faraday)
poloidal flux $\psi$$0 = \frac{1}{r} \partial_r (r\, \partial_r \psi) - 2\pi \mu_0 j_\mathrm{tot}$ (Ampère)
plasma current $I_p$$0 = \sum 2\pi r\, \Delta r\, j_\mathrm{tot} - I_p$
flux at the edge $\psi_\mathrm{edge}$$0 = \psi_\mathrm{wall} - \mu_0 \ln(b/a)\, I_p - \psi_\mathrm{edge}$
flux at the wall $\psi_\mathrm{wall}$$\mathrm{d}\psi_\mathrm{wall}/\mathrm{d}t = V_\mathrm{loop,wall}$

The row of the field holds the time derivative of the flux (TimeDerivative with of). Ampère's law is PoloidalFluxDiffusionTerm, with the gradient at the edge from $\psi_\mathrm{edge}$. The plasma current is CrossSectionIntegralTerm, and the inductance between the edge and the wall edge_to_wall_inductance. A loop voltage of zero at the wall is a perfectly conducting wall. The caller gives the row of $j_\mathrm{tot}$, the sum of the currents of the run. initial_poloidal_flux solves Ampère's law for the initial current density, as DREAM does.
