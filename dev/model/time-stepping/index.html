Time stepping and solvers

A run is one call of integrate on an EquationSystem, the system $M\,\mathrm{d}u/\mathrm{d}t = \mathrm{rhs}(u, t)$ of the page Unknowns and the equation system. Three levels nest in it. A TimeStepper sets the length of every step and the number of solves it takes. Each solve is one call of newton_solve! with the settings of a NewtonSolver. Each Newton iteration is one linear solve by a LinearSolver. Between two accepted steps the run saves states, tests the stop, writes checkpoints and applies its interval updates. The settings of a run can be passed as keywords or as one RunOptions.

The step

Every step is a backward-Euler step from the state $u_n$ at $t_n$ to $u$ at $t = t_n + h$, the zero of the residual of residual!:

\[F(u) = \frac{M\,(u - u_\mathrm{ref})}{h} - \mathrm{rhs}(u, t),\]

with $u_\mathrm{ref} = u_n$. The two-step BDF2 of the step controller passes another reference and an effective step (Step controller). The terms of the system enter the step in three groups:

  • implicit terms with a stored Jacobian, evaluated at $u$;
  • implicit matrix-free terms, evaluated at $u$ and entering the Jacobian only through products (jacobian_operator);
  • explicit terms, evaluated at $u_n$ and absent from the Jacobian.

A term can also lag part of its residual to the start of the step, which prepare_step! sets from $u_n$. The ion densities in the collisions of a FokkerPlanckTerm (lagged) and the primary distribution of a KnockOnTerm (treatment = :explicit_primary) are lagged this way. A lagged part is first-order accurate in the step, also under BDF2 (FokkerPlanckTerm, StepController).

The same system can be driven by OrdinaryDiffEq or NonlinearSolve through ode_function. In that form there is no start of the step, so a lagged part is evaluated at the state itself, and ode_function passes no Jacobian for a system with such a term (lags_in_ode_form).

Newton iteration

newton_solve! iterates on $F(u) = 0$ from the guess $u = u_n$. The linear system of an iteration is scaled by rows and columns:

\[A = W \left(\frac{M}{h} - J\right) S, \qquad A\,\tilde\delta = -W F, \qquad \delta = S\,\tilde\delta,\]

with $J$ the Jacobian of the implicit terms, $S$ the scales of the unknowns and $W$ the row weights. A row with a time derivative has the weight $h/s_i$, an algebraic row $1/s_i$, with $s_i$ the scale of the row. On a row with a time derivative the scaled residual $|W F|$ is then the change that the residual causes in one step relative to the scale of the unknown. On an algebraic row it is the residual relative to the scale of the row.

Convergence

An iterate is accepted when two tests pass:

  • the update: every entry satisfies $|\delta_i| \le a_i + r_i\,|u_i|$ with the Newton Tolerance of its unknown (tolerance);
  • the residual: $\max_i |W F|_i$ is at most residual_tolerance.

The group part of a Tolerance adds group times the sum of the charge-state densities of the species at the radius, so that a trace impurity is converged on its own scale. The unknown of a Constraint is a rate, and its update test admits the round-off of the constrained quantity divided by $h$. An unknown with an infinite absolute tolerance is left out of both tests.

Two waivers of the residual test exist, each flagged in the NewtonResult and the SolverStatistics:

  • the residual floor: on a stiff step the transient and the right-hand side cancel to many digits, and round-off holds the residual above residual_tolerance. A step whose update passes is accepted once the residual stops decreasing (residual_floor).
  • the linear step: a system linear in the state (is_linear) is solved by the first iteration and accepted at the second, on the update test alone (linear_step, linear_acceptance).

Both waivers need an exact Newton matrix and a linear solve accurate to floor_linear_tolerance. An inexact solve or an approximate Jacobian block (jacobian_kind) converges linearly, with an update that understates the error. Such a quasi-Newton iteration is held by the residual test alone.

For a system linear in the state the stored Jacobian depends only on the end time and the length of the step. With reuse_linear_matrix it is assembled and factorized once per step and kept for the later iterations and for a repeated step. An update of the setup discards it (invalidate_jacobian!).

Lower limits and line search

A LowerLimit declared on an unknown shortens the whole Newton step, so that no limited entry loses more than the fraction max_reduction of its distance to its limit in one iteration. Steps away from the limit are never shortened. The default of max_reduction and its origin in DREAM are on the page Positivity and flux limiters. The temperature of a PlasmaFromUnknowns has the floor of that source as its limit. A temperature that only fluid terms read takes the default floor DEFAULT_TEMPERATURE_FLOOR as its limit, unless it declares a LowerLimit of its own (fluid_temperature_floors). A converged state below a limit is refused with the status :below_lower_limit. The limiters of the advection are on the page Positivity and flux limiters.

line_search = true adds backtracking on $\tfrac12 \|W F\|^2$ with the Armijo condition, starting from the step length the lower limits allow. It is off by default.

A failed iteration never throws. Its reason is the status of the NewtonResult, and the stepper retries the step with a shorter length.

Linear solvers and preconditioners

SolverMethodImplicit matrix-free terms
SparseLUsparse LU (UMFPACK) of the whole matrix, the symbolic analysis reusedlow-rank ones only, by a Woodbury update
GMRESrestarted GMRES (Krylov.jl), right preconditionedall, through their products

SparseLU is the default. It takes the field-particle term (low_rank_jacobian) exactly and refuses a system with other implicit matrix-free terms, such as the implicit knock-on term.

GMRES solves with the operator $W (M/h - J) S$ in which $J$ holds the stored Jacobian and the matrix-free terms. With right preconditioning the residual it minimizes is the true residual of the scaled system. Its relative_tolerance must be at or below the floor_linear_tolerance of the NewtonSolver, or neither waiver can apply. operator = :exact adds the corrections of approximate Jacobian blocks to every product (jacobian_correction_product!), so the iteration is Newton and the approximate block only preconditions. operator = :stored keeps the stored matrix, a quasi-Newton iteration. The default restart length of 50 is a choice of this package (Choices of this package).

The preconditioners are factorizations of the stored matrix in blocks (BlockPreconditioner):

  • RadiusBlockLU and RadiusBlockILU: one block per radius (radius_block_range) and one for the scalars (scalar_block_range), each factorized exactly or incompletely. The couplings between the blocks, the radial transport and the couplings to the scalars, are left out of the preconditioner and left to the Krylov iteration.
  • FluidSchurLU, the default: the kinetic unknowns of each radius as blocks, and the fluid unknowns of all radii in one block through its Schur complement. Where every kinetic unknown has one pitch grid at all radii, tridiagonal line solves along the radii take the radial transport of the distributions (radial_lines). Without them the radial transport is left to the Krylov iteration. It holds the coupling through Ampère's law and the scalars exactly.

preconditioner_refresh sets when the factors are renewed: in every Newton iteration, in the first iteration of every step, or on demand when the step changes or the Krylov count grows. A solve that fails with older factors is repeated once with fresh ones.

Time steppers

The stepper sets the length of every step. Every time point of the run is the end of a step, and every saved state is a computed one.

StepperError estimateStep lengthSave times
FixedSteps (default)noneone step per interval of the time points, halved on failuretime points
StepDoublingtwo half steps against one full step$\text{safety}\cdot h/\text{error}^{\text{exponent}}$time points

A step that fails is retried with a shorter step, up to a set number of times, after which the run throws TimeStepFailure. The state a stepper carries between steps is in every checkpoint (stepper_state).

Fixed steps and step doubling

FixedSteps is DREAM's constant stepper. A failed step is retried with half the time step, the shorter steps continue to the end of the interval, and the next interval starts again with its full length.

StepDoubling is DREAM's adaptive stepper, the compatibility option of the step control. The error of an unknown is $\|u_\mathrm{full} - u_\mathrm{half}\|_2 / (a + r\,\|u_\mathrm{full}\|_2)$, with $u_\mathrm{half}$ the result of the two half steps and the norms taken over all entries of the unknown at every radius. The error of the step is the largest over the unknowns, and the step is accepted at an error of at most 1. Its tolerances are the time_step_tolerances of the EquationSystem; an unknown without one gets relative_tolerance and no absolute part, and an unknown with both tolerances zero is left out.

Step controller

StepController estimates the local error of a backward-Euler step from the linear extrapolation through the two last accepted states:

\[P = u_n + \frac{h}{h_{n-1}}\,(u_n - u_{n-1}), \qquad \mathrm{err} = \tfrac12\,(u - P).\]

On the rows with a time derivative, where $M\,(u_n - u_{n-1}) = h_{n-1}\,\mathrm{rhs}(u_n)$, $P$ is the forward-Euler predictor, and $u - P \approx h^2 u''$ is twice the local error. The first step of a run, and the first after an update that changed the setup, has no history and takes its estimate from one step doubling.

The error of an unknown is the root mean square of the estimate over $a + r\,|u|$ per entry:

  • an unknown without its own Tolerance gets relative_tolerance and the absolute part relative_tolerance times its scale, a floor for entries near zero;
  • the charge states of an ion species are measured against the density of their species (component_groups);
  • a kinetic unknown also holds its density and current density above the momentum tail_cut at every radius (density, current_density with a MomentumRegion).

The values of a distribution in its hot tail lie far below the absolute tolerance of the cell error, relative_tolerance times the scale, so the cells alone leave the tail uncontrolled; the tail moments control it. Their absolute tolerance is tail_floor times the density of the electrons on all kinetic grids at the radius, which stays a floor for a runaway grid whose electrons all lie above the cut.

The next step follows the PI controller of K. Gustafsson, M. Lundh and G. Söderlind, BIT 28, 270 (1988), doi:10.1007/BF01934091:

\[h_\mathrm{new} = h \cdot \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\text{safety}\cdot \mathrm{err}^{-k_1}\,\mathrm{err}_\mathrm{old}^{\,k_2},\ \text{min\_shrink},\ \text{max\_growth}\right),\]

with the gains $(k_1, k_2) = (0.7, 0.4)/(q + 1)$ for a method of order $q$, those of K. Gustafsson, ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 17, 533 (1991), doi:10.1145/210232.210242. Both are in section IV.2 of E. Hairer and G. Wanner, Solving Ordinary Differential Equations II: Stiff and Differential-Algebraic Problems, 2nd ed., Springer (1996), doi:10.1007/978-3-642-05221-7. A rejected step is repeated with a shorter step and keeps the memory of the filter. A step cut short by a time point or a save time sets the next length from the step taken.

With order = 2 the steps are those of the variable-step BDF2, with $\omega = h/h_{n-1}$:

\[M\,(u - a\,u_n + b\,u_{n-1}) = c\,h\,\mathrm{rhs}(u, t), \qquad a = \frac{(1+\omega)^2}{1+2\omega}, \quad b = \frac{\omega^2}{1+2\omega}, \quad c = \frac{1+\omega}{1+2\omega}.\]

The solve is the backward-Euler residual with $u_\mathrm{ref} = a\,u_n - b\,u_{n-1}$ and the step $c\,h$. The error estimate is Milne's, from the quadratic extrapolation through the three last accepted states. The first two steps of a run, and the first two after an update that changes the setup, are backward-Euler steps. BDF2 is zero-stable for $\omega < 1 + \sqrt2$, which bounds max_growth. It keeps neither the positivity that backward Euler has with an M-matrix operator nor the second order for explicit and lagged terms. The default order = 1 is backward Euler, which DREAM uses.

The estimate assumes that every term is implicit. An explicit or lagged term contributes $h\,h_{n-1}\,u''/2$ in place of $h^2 u''/2$, a factor $h_{n-1}/h$ that the limits of the step ratio bound.

Legs

Legs describe the time points of a run in pieces, each with its steps and its saved states. time_points turns a list of legs into the times and save_times of integrate with the same floating-point sums, so that every save time of a prescribed-step leg is exactly a time point. A leg with save_dt alone has no prescribed steps and serves a StepController.

Interval updates

An IntervalUpdate changes the setup of the run at the start and after every accepted step, at the state and the time where that step ends. The step that follows uses the new setup.

  • KnockOnCutoffUpdate moves the knock-on cutoff of each radius with the plasma.
  • RechesterRosenbluthUpdate sets the Rechester–Rosenbluth diffusion (add_rechester_rosenbluth_diffusion!) from the prescribed $\delta B/B$ at the start of each step. The diffusion is that of A. B. Rechester and M. N. Rosenbluth, Phys. Rev. Lett. 40, 38 (1978), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.40.38.

An update that reports a changed setup discards the stored Jacobian and the history of the step controller. A time at which an update or a term switches (forced_time_points) must be a time point of the run, and check_update refuses an update that does not fit the system. The state of every update is saved with every saved state and checkpoint, and restored on resume.

Checkpoints, segments and the stop

A run that does not fit one allocation is a chain of segments. After each accepted step the loop does, in order:

  1. the saves at the end of the step;
  2. the stop test of the StopCondition: the wall time left or a stop file (stop_reason);
  3. a checkpoint (CheckpointSettings) when its interval has passed, at a stop and at the last time point;
  4. the interval updates for the next step.

A checkpoint writes the output of the segment first and the checkpoint file after it, each by write_atomically. It holds the state, the states of the updates, the record of the stepper and a summary of the run (StoredState). resume continues from a stored state (read_checkpoint, read_state), and resume = true lets one script serve every segment. On the same layout and with a direct solver a resumed run repeats the uninterrupted one bit for bit. With GMRES(preconditioner_refresh = :on_demand) the preconditioner factors are not stored, so the Krylov solves after a resume differ within the Newton tolerance, and so may the step lengths of a StepController. A run resumed onto another layout starts the step controller with a doubling, and fills only the unknowns with initial_value = :from_equation that the stored state does not have (fill_initial_values!).

run_status and exit_status tell a batch script whether another segment follows. read_output joins the segments, and the file format is on the page Output files. A ProgressLog reports the run while it goes: a header, entries at milestones of the simulated time, and the events of failed steps, checkpoints and stops.

Comparison with DREAM

test/test_time_stepping_dream.jl makes two comparisons with DREAM:

  • multi-step backward Euler of the Fokker-Planck operator with DREAM's explicit knock-on term on a prescribed constant plasma, over DREAM's time points. It covers two collision variants (superthermal and full), each with SparseLU and with GMRES.
  • StepDoubling against DREAM's adaptive stepper: the accepted steps, the errors and the rejected attempts, and the moments n_hot, j_hot and n_re at DREAM's accepted times. They are compared over the stretch before DREAM's step update reads the tolerances of the wrong unknowns. The errors of a second DREAM run, with the check of the runaway unknowns off, are compared over the same stretch.

The departures of the step control and of the Newton iteration from DREAM are on the page Comparison with DREAM.
